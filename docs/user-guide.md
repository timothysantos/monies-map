# User guide

Everything you need to use Monie's Map, from your first visit to closing a
bank statement. New here? Read **Start here** first, then jump to any recipe
under **How do I…**. Tap a picture to open it full size. Technical setup lives
in the For developers tab.

# Start here

## What is Monie's Map?

Monie's Map is a household money planner and tracker for two people. It keeps
your plan, your real spending, your bank statements and your shared expenses
in one place, so you can answer five questions every month:

- What did we intend to spend and save?
- What actually happened?
- Was the difference justified?
- Did it hurt our savings?
- Which assumption was wrong?

It does not connect to your bank. You add spending by hand, from Apple Pay, or
by importing the files your bank lets you download. A bank statement then
proves the numbers are right.

## Take the 3-minute tour

The navigation at the top (on a phone, at the bottom) has six screens. They
appear in this order:

1. **Summary**: the big picture for a range of months: planned versus actual
   income, spending and savings, where the money went, and your wallet
   balances. This is **All months**.
   ![Summary in Plan shows planned and actual totals, money insights, spending mix and intent versus outcome](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-household.webp)
   The pictures in this guide show **Plan**; Summary and Month open in
   **Simple** until you choose (see **Plan | Simple** under
   [Summary](#summary)).
   Pick one month of the range to see its plan (the **Month** page): income,
   monthly budgets and bills and dated payments next to what was actually spent, and
   where that month's money went ([Pick a month](#pick-a-month)).
   ![Month shows the month's totals, money insights and Spending Mix, with the plan rows below](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/month-person.webp)
2. **Entries**: every transaction for the month, grouped by day, with search
   and filters. This is where you add, fix and categorise spending.
   ![Entries lists transactions by day with totals and filters](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/entries-person.webp)
3. **Splits**: shared expenses between the two of you: who paid, who owes
   whom, and settling up. It has a dark orange look so you always know you are
   in Splits.
   ![Splits uses a dark orange theme and shows groups, balances and shared expenses](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-person.webp)
4. **Imports**: bring in bank files. Drop them at the top; the Import inbox
   tells you which files to download from which bank.
   ![Imports starts with the Import inbox, which plans your bank downloads](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/imports-inbox.webp)
5. **Settings**: people, accounts, categories, category rules, Apple Pay and
   the tools that keep balances trustworthy.
   ![Settings groups everything into sections you open one at a time](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/settings-overview.webp)
6. **FAQ**: this guide.

On a phone there is one more: **Ask**, where you add an entry by typing it,
from a receipt photo, or by filling in a card, and find entries by asking
for them ([Ask](#ask)). On a computer, Ask is the box at the bottom right of
every screen, **Ask, or drop a receipt…**: click it, or press **/**, and Ask
opens beside the screen you are on ([Ask on a computer](#ask-on-a-computer)).

At the top left you choose whose money you are looking at (Household, or one
person). At the top right you move the range of months (on Entries, the
month) and hide or show money totals. Imports, Settings and FAQ do not
depend on a month, so they show no month or range controls there.

## The core idea: plan, record, import, reconcile, split

Monie's Map works best as a monthly loop:

1. **Plan**: on Month (pick the month on Summary), set what you expect to earn, your fixed commitments
   (bills and dated payments) and flexible budgets (monthly budgets).
2. **Record**: as you spend, add entries on Entries, or let Apple Pay create
   them for you.
3. **Import**: during the month, import your bank's activity export to catch
   everything you missed.
4. **Reconcile**: when the statement arrives, import the PDF. It confirms each
   row and checks that the balance matches the bank to the cent.
5. **Split**: anything you share goes on Splits, so you always know who owes
   whom, and you settle up when you are ready.

Summary then shows whether the month went as planned. You do not have to do
every step: many households only plan, record and split.

## Views: Household, Ethan and Serene

The switch at the top left changes whose money you see. The names come from
Settings → People (the demo uses Ethan and Serene).

- **Household** shows everything, for both people together. The Month plan is
  read-only here, because each person keeps their own plan and the household
  plan is the two combined.
- **A person** (for example Ethan) shows that person's own money: their own
  entries, plus their share of anything on Splits. This is where you edit that
  person's plan.
- **Splits always uses a person.** It opens on the person linked to your
  sign-in (or the first person), and the Household button is greyed out there,
  because "you owe" only makes sense for one person.

![A person view shows that person's figures and the scope choice under the heading](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-person.webp)

![In the Household view, Month says the combined plan is read-only](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/month-household.webp)

The view is kept in the address, so a bookmarked page reopens the same way.

## Scopes: Direct, Shared, Direct + Shared

In a person view, the scope buttons narrow what counts:

- **Direct ownership**: the person's own entries that are not on Splits.
- **Shared**: only their share of expenses that are on Splits.
- **Direct + Shared** (the default): both together. This is their real
  spending.

A worked example: Ethan pays $100.00 for groceries and $80.00 for a dinner
split 25% Ethan / 75% Serene. Serene pays $30.00 for shopping.

- Ethan, Direct + Shared: $100.00 + $20.00 = **$120.00**
- Serene, Direct + Shared: $30.00 + $60.00 = **$90.00**
- Household: **$210.00** (every entry once)
- Ethan, Shared only: **$20.00**

Summary, Month and Entries all count the same way, so the numbers agree
everywhere. The Household view has no scope buttons because it always counts
every entry once. On a phone, Summary, Month and Entries keep the view and
scope in the floating **View and scope** bar ([On your phone](#on-your-phone)).
On a desktop, a compact bar returns at the bottom left after you scroll past
the header, aligned with the Ask box. Its tabs stay on the left, followed by
the view, scope and month controls, so you can move between Summary, Entries,
Splits and Ask or change the current view without going back to the top. On a
long page, a translucent arrow briefly appears at the bottom centre while you
scroll down to take you to the bottom; scrolling up changes it to the shortcut
back to the top.

## Hide or show money totals

Totals start hidden on every new browser, so you can open the app around other
people. Every amount shows as `••••`: totals, transactions, splits, charts and
plan amounts, without its colour or any words that say whether it is up or
down (such as "Planned more than you earn" or "You owe"). Amount fields you type into are
masked too.

![With totals hidden, every amount shows as dots](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/money-hidden.webp)

1. Select the **eye button** at the top right (on a phone, the round floating
   eye button above the bottom navigation).
2. Amounts appear everywhere at once. Select it again to hide them.

The choice is remembered only in that browser. A different browser or a
cleared browser starts hidden again. Money insights stay hidden while
totals are hidden, because their sentences would reveal the same figures;
they appear with the real figures as soon as you reveal totals. The earned,
spent and kept bar keeps its shape and its key shows only the names and
percentages (no amounts, and no plan) while totals are hidden, and each line under **Where your money goes** shows only ✓ or the share of its plan
reached. Edit
forms also have a small eye button next to an amount, so you can check a
single figure without revealing the whole page.

> Tip: you can still import a bank file while totals are hidden. The file's
> raw text is screened until you reveal totals.

## Try it safely with the demo data

The demo household (Ethan and Serene, with bank accounts, credit cards and
investment accounts, 17 months of plans and bank statements, and shared
expenses including a trip to Japan) is the best way to learn. Everything in this guide was captured from
it.

On the demo site, open **Settings** and use **Demo state**:

- **Reseed demo data** replaces everything with this site's demo data,
  so the sample data is back exactly as it was.
- **Enter empty state** clears all money data (accounts, entries, imports,
  plans, splits) and keeps only the people and categories, so you can start
  fresh. You type `empty state` to confirm.
- **Reload app data** only reloads what is on screen.

These buttons are not shown on the real (private) app.

## On your phone

The app is designed for phones as well as desktops. The differences:

- **Bottom navigation.** Summary, Entries, Splits and Ask sit at the
  bottom, with Ask in your accent colour and bolder so it is easy to find.
  The **•••** button opens Imports, Settings and FAQ.
  ![On a phone, the ••• button opens Imports, Settings and FAQ](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-more.webp)
- **View and scope bar.** On Summary, Month and Entries, a floating bar above
  the navigation shows who and what you are viewing (for example
  `Ethan · Direct + Shared`), with the person in their own colour. Tap it to
  change the view first, then the scope; on Entries the sheet also has the
  filters, with **Reset filters** and **Done** at the bottom. Swipe the sheet
  down to close it. Entries adds previous and next month buttons beside the
  bar.
  ![The floating View and scope bar opens a sheet to change person and scope](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-view-scope.webp)
  When you filter Entries, this bar names the filters and how many entries
  match. Tap a search summary to go straight to the search box, or a category
  summary to open the category choices. Tap the person chip to change view or
  scope. **Clear** removes all filters at once. Your person, month and scope stay. The usual **Direct + Shared**
  words make room for the filters and return after Clear. **Direct** or
  **Shared** stays visible, with the filters on another row.
  When viewing an older month, **Back to this month** returns to the current
  month if it is within your chosen range. Splits' floating group bar also
  offers **Clear** while you search that group.
  On a computer, the floating bar also keeps the page tabs. Entries names the
  chosen month between its arrows. Summary and Month offer **Plan | Simple**
  there as well as on the main card. On Splits, it shows groups with open
  entries and their counts; **All groups** still shows every group. Settled
  batches stay together behind the **Archived batches** row.

- **Month button.** On Summary, the button beside the bar says **All
  months**. Tap it to pick one month of the range
  ([Pick a month](#pick-a-month)). With a month picked it shows that month,
  with **‹** and **›** to step to the month before or after.
- **Edit sheets.** Adding or editing a row opens a sheet from the bottom of
  the screen instead of editing inside the table. **Done** or **Save** saves;
  **Cancel**, the ✕ or tapping outside closes it without saving. You can also
  swipe the sheet down to close it (the small bar at the top shows where to
  grab). If you have changed something, it springs back and asks
  **Discard changes?**: **Keep editing** takes you back with your changes,
  **Discard** closes it without saving. While a save is running, a swipe does
  nothing. On Month the
  sheet starts with the planned amount: tap it to type a new one. Under it, a
  bar shows what has been spent against the plan and what is left (or how
  much it is over). The other fields are rows below it: tap a row to change
  it, or **See the entries** to open the entries behind the actual. **Done**
  and **Cancel** are at the bottom, in reach of your thumb.
  ![Editing a plan row on a phone opens a sheet](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-month-sheet.webp)
- **Floating buttons.** The round **+** button on Entries opens a new entry
  card on Ask; on Splits, the receipt button opens one that is already
  shared on Splits. The eye button hides or shows totals. While you are moving
  through a long Summary, Month, Entries or Splits page, an arrow briefly
  appears: it takes you back to the top after scrolling down, or to the end
  after scrolling up.

# The screens

Each screen in the order it appears in the navigation: what you see, what
each control does, the common tasks, and what changes on a phone.

Money colours mean the same on every screen: olive green is money coming in
or something going well, brick red is money going out, over plan, short or
owed by you, teal is a plan, and transfers stay in plain text. In lists only the
amount's text is coloured; Summary's spending turns red only when you are
over plan.

## Summary

### What you see

- **Spent**: what you spent over the selected range against what you planned
  to spend, with the share of the plan used and how much you are under
  (green) or over (red) plan. Money you put into **Investments** or
  **Savings** is not counted as spending here, and your savings target and
  investment plan are not part of the spending plan, so this always agrees
  with the "Spend within" line under **Where your money goes** below.
- **Earned, spent and kept**: under the Spent figure, one bar stands for all
  the money you earned over the range. The red part is what you spent, the
  plum part is what you invested (entries in the **Investments** category,
  and money you moved into an investment account, less what you took back
  out of one) and the green part is what you kept. A transfer between two
  of your investment accounts, or between two bank accounts, changes
  nothing here. If you took more out of your investments than you put in,
  invested shows nothing and the key adds a line such as **Took out of
  investments $2,500**: that money is not counted as income or as kept. Money you filed under **Savings** is
  counted as kept, not spent. If more went out than you earned, the bar
  runs past a small dark mark where your income ends, named in the key
  (**All you earned** with the amount on a wider screen, **Earned** on a
  phone), and the striped part after it is the money that came out of your
  savings. A thin blue bar under it shows your plan on the same scale: what
  you planned to spend, to invest and to save. Under the bars a short key is
  always there, with no hovering: a colour square and the amount for each
  part ("Spent $20,900", "Kept $38,000", "From savings $54,871"; a wider
  screen adds the share of what you earned), then the thin bar, named
  **Plan** (a wider screen adds the plan: "Plan: spend $24,767 · keep
  $4,130"). One line says it in words ("You kept $12,000 and invested $8,000
  of the $247,000 you earned"; on a phone, "Kept $12,000 · invested $8,000 of
  $247,000"). Click a part of the bar, a line of the key or the sentence (on
  a phone, tap it) to see where it came from ([Dig into any
  figure](#dig-into-any-figure)).
- **Where your money goes**: each line of your plan has a purpose: spend within the
  budget, save the savings target, invest what you planned to invest, and
  the income you expected. One line says how many were on track ("3 of 4
  things on track"); open it (on a phone, tap it) to see each one with **✓ done**,
  **short by** or **over by** an amount. While the month is still going it
  says "so far". Select a line for **View entries**, **Open plan** and **Ask
  about this**. It only looks back at what happened against your own plan;
  it never guesses what is still to come.
- **Income** and **Saved**: the income that came in, with the planned income
  under it, and what you kept (the same as the green part of the bar, so
  money you filed under **Savings** counts as saved), with your savings
  target under it.
- **Money insights**: one thing worth knowing about the figures on screen
  ([Use Money insights](#use-money-insights)).
- **Spending Mix**: a donut of spending by category over the whole range,
  with the categories ranked beside it (under it on a phone): each shows its
  amount, its share and a thin bar. The bigger slices are named on the donut
  with their share where there is room; small slices are only in the list. The top five show; **Show all** lists
  the rest. Pick a month to see that month's ([Month](#month)).
- **Intent vs Outcome**: one card per month, newest first. Each leads with
  what you spent against what you planned, with a bar, the percent of plan
  and how far under or over you are. Your planned and actual income sit
  under it, and **Details** opens the income, spending and savings figures.
- **The accounts shown**: one list with each account's current balance and
  whether a statement has confirmed it. Select an account to open its entries.
  An investment account you have imported an iFAST or StashAway statement for says what
  its latest statement said it was worth, with the statement's date ("$2,023.90 on 30 Sep 2026, as the statement says"). That is the statement's
  figure on that day, not a forecast and not money you can spend; the
  balance beside it is still what you moved in
  ([Import an investment statement](#import-an-investment-statement-ifast-and-stashaway)).
  Hide money totals and both the figure and the sentence go.

![Spending Mix: the donut beside the ranked categories, with Intent vs Outcome below](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-charts.webp)

### What each control does

- **Plan | Simple** (top right of the Spent card): **Plan** shows your
  spending against your plan, as above. **Simple** is for when you do not
  keep a plan, and changes the whole page: the card shows only what you
  **Earned** and what you **Spent**, the same bar without the plan, one
  line in words ("You kept $12,000 of the $80,000 you earned (15%)" or "You
  spent $300 more than you earned"), and a few facts: your **daily spend**
  and your **top spending** categories with their share of your spending
  (select one to see its entries). The figures are the same in both.
  **Earned vs spent by month** takes the place of Intent vs Outcome: one row
  per month, newest first, with what you kept (or overspent), what you
  earned and spent, and a thin bar; the month's name opens that month.
  When Summary covers two or more months, a chart sits above those rows (see
  **The chart above the months** below).
  Money insights leave out the ones about your plan. Until you choose,
  Summary and Month open in **Simple**, as in the picture below; the other
  pictures in this guide show **Plan**. Your choice is remembered for your
  login, on every phone and computer you use, for Summary and Month alike
  (in the demo, on this device). You can also set it in
  [Settings](#settings) under **Summary view**.

  ![Summary in Simple: what you earned and spent, the bar, and a few facts](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-simple.webp)

- **The chart above the months**: when Summary covers two or more months,
  the card with the month-by-month list (**Earned vs spent by month** in
  Simple, **Intent vs Outcome** in Plan) starts with a chart. Each month
  has two columns, **Earned** and **Spent**, and a line runs across the
  months. In Simple the line is what you **Kept**; it drops below the zero
  line in a month you spent more than you earned. In Plan the line is your
  **Planned spending**, so a month where the Spent column rises above the
  line went over plan; a month with no plan has no point on the line. The
  columns grow up and the line draws itself the first time you scroll to
  it (not at all if your phone is set to reduce motion). Select a column or
  a point, or on a computer rest the pointer on it, to see that month's
  figure with a few facts, then choose **View entries** for the entries
  behind it, **Open plan** (Plan only) or **Ask about this**. The arrow keys
  move between them. Select a name above the chart (**Earned**, **Spent**,
  **Kept** or **Planned spending**) to hide or show it. Hide money totals
  and the chart goes away, and with a single month there is no chart.

  ![Earned vs spent by month starts with a chart of each month's earned and spent and a line for what was kept](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-month-chart.webp)

- **Range** (top right): the months included, 12 by default. Use **‹** and
  **›** to move the whole range, or select the start or end month to pick one.
  ![Selecting the start of the range opens a month picker](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-range-picker.webp)
- **Scope** (person views only): the small **Direct / Shared / Both** switch
  above the **Income** and **Saved** cards on a computer. Hover an option to
  see what it counts; one click switches
  ([Scopes](#scopes-direct-shared-direct-shared)).
- **Month** (on a computer, the row of months under the top bar; on a
  phone, the button beside the View and scope bar): **All months** is this
  page. Pick a month to open that month's plan
  ([Pick a month](#pick-a-month)).
- **The donut**: each slice with room is named with its share. Point at a
  slice (on a phone, tap it; with the keyboard, Tab to the donut and use the
  arrow keys, then Enter) for its category name (first, in bold, with its
  colour; its row in the list is marked while the menu is open), its amount,
  share and number of entries, then
  **View entries** and **Ask about this** ([Dig into any
  figure](#dig-into-any-figure)). **Hide list** folds the list away so the
  donut fills the card; **Show list** brings it back. Your choice is kept on
  this device.
- **A category in the list**: select its name to hide or show it in the donut
  (a hidden one is dimmed and says Hidden). The list shows the top five (three
  on a phone). The coloured dot changes the category's icon and colour. The
  **›** beside it shows where
  that category's spending came from: **View entries** lists it for the
  whole range, adding up to the amount shown. **Show all** lists every
  category;
  **Show fewer** folds it back. With money hidden the amounts show as dots and
  the percentages stay.
- **A month card** in Intent vs Outcome: select **Details** for the planned,
  actual and variance figures (the latest month starts open). The **Note** row
  there edits that month's note. The month's name opens that month.

![The accounts shown, with current balances and statement status](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-wallets.webp)

### Common tasks

1. To see one person: select their name at the top left.
2. To check last year: select **‹** next to the range until the months you
   want are shown.
3. To find what drove spending: select the Spent figure, then **View
   entries**, or select **›** next to the biggest category in Spending Mix,
   or pick a month to see its plan.

### Pick a month

Summary and Month share one place in the navigation. **All months** shows
Summary for the whole range; one month shows that month's plan (Month). The
months you can pick are the months in the range at the top; change the range
there to reach other months.

- On a computer, select a month in the row under the top bar, or **All
  months** to come back.
- On a phone, tap **All months** (or the month's name) beside the View and
  scope bar. **Pick a month** opens: **All months** with what was spent
  against the plan for the whole range, then each month with what was spent
  and a bar against its plan. A month that went over its plan shows its
  amount in red with "over". The month you are on has an orange border and a
  tick. **Change range** takes you to the range at the top.
  ![Pick a month on a phone: All months, then each month with what was spent against its plan](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-month-picker.webp)
- With a month picked, **‹** and **›** beside its name step to the month
  before or after, within the range.
- The **Summary** button in the navigation always goes back to All months.

With totals hidden, the amounts show as dots, the bar turns grey and shows
only percentages, and nothing turns red or says "under" or "over".

### Dig into any figure

Every amount and money line on a card can be opened to see where it came
from: Spent and its plan, each part of the earned, spent and kept bar and
each line of its key, the sentence under it, Where your money goes, Income and
Saved, Daily spend and Top spending, each month in Intent vs Outcome and in
Earned vs spent by month, the Spending Mix categories, the Month plan's
actual amounts, the Entries totals and the Splits balance. Nothing looks
like a button: point at a figure (or move to it with Tab) and it is
underlined with a small arrow after it; select it (on a phone, tap it) and
a small menu opens under it with what applies:

- **View entries** opens Entries showing only the entries behind that
  figure, for the same months, person and scope, and the total at the top
  is the same figure, to the cent. From a range of months it lists the
  whole range ("Invested · Jun 2025 – May 2026"). Invested, for example,
  lists your **Investments** entries and the money you moved into an
  investment account, less what you took back out. A line at the top of the
  list names what it shows; select **✕** on it to see the whole month again.
- **Open plan** opens that month's plan on the Month page (for plan
  figures and Where your money goes).
- **Open Splits** opens that group on Splits (for a split balance).
- **Ask about this** opens Ask, asks a question about it for you and shows
  the answer.

Press **Esc** (or tap anywhere else) to close the menu. With totals hidden
the menu is the same, and its question never says whether you were under
or over.

Two figures are not a list of entries as they are: **Daily spend** is an
average, so **View entries** shows the spending it is worked out from; and
a budget line whose refunds are more than its spending shows $0 actual,
while its entries add up to the refund.

### On a phone

Spent sits on top, with Income and Saved side by side under it. The range
sits at the top. The person and scope
live in the floating **View and scope** bar above the navigation; tap it to
change either. The month button beside it picks a month
([Pick a month](#pick-a-month)).

## Month

Month is the plan for one month. Open it from Summary by picking a month
([Pick a month](#pick-a-month)); the **Summary** button in the navigation
stays highlighted, and the range at the top stays as it was.

### What you see

- **Header**: the month, the person, the scope buttons and **Actions**.
- **The top card**: what you have spent this month against what you
  planned to spend, with the percent of plan and how much you are under or
  over plan (investing and saving are not spending, as on Summary).
  Under that is the same earned, spent and kept bar as on
  [Summary](#summary), for this month, and its Where your money goes line ("2 of 3
  things on track", folded on a phone). Under it, in the same card, are **Income** (received, with
  the plan), **Saved** (with the savings target) and **Not planned yet**
  (planned income minus planned spend; it says **Planned more than you earn** when negative).
  Select it to see what it means, with **Open plan** and **Ask about this**.
- **Money insights** for the month.
- **Spending Mix**: the month's spending by category, the same donut and
  ranked list as on Summary. Select a category to hide or show it in the donut; its
  **›** offers that category's entries for this month. A month with no
  spending says so instead of showing an empty donut.
- **Income**: planned income sources. On every plan row a thin bar shows how
  much of the plan you have used. On a phone each row is a small card; tap it
  to change it.
- **Monthly budget**: flexible budgets by category, such as Groceries or Taxi.
  Their actual comes from all entries in that category.
- **Bills and dated payments**: fixed commitments such as savings, insurance, loans and
  subscriptions. Their actual comes from the entries you link to them.
- **Monthly Note**: why the month looked the way it did.
- **Accounts**: the tracked accounts.

### What each control does

- **Plan | Simple** (top right of the top card): the same switch as on
  [Summary](#summary). Simple shows what you earned and spent this month,
  with the bar and the same few facts; in place of the plan rows it lists
  **Money in** (what came in, one row per source; select one to see its
  entries), and **Plan this month** switches back to Plan to set monthly budgets
  and dated payments. Spending Mix shows where the money went in both.
  Month opens in Simple until you choose Plan.
- **Month**: the row of months under the top bar (on a phone, **‹**, the
  month's name and **›** beside the View and scope bar) moves to another
  month of the range, or **All months** back to Summary.
- **Select a row** (anywhere outside its buttons) to edit it in place:
  category, item name, planned amount and note. **Save** keeps it, **Cancel**
  discards, **Delete** removes the row.
  ![Selecting a monthly budget opens it for editing in the table](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/month-edit-row.webp)
- **+ Add income source**, **+ Add monthly budget**, **+ Add bill or dated payment**:
  add a row to that section.
- **Link entries** (under a bill or dated payment's actual): choose which entries pay
  for it ([Link bills and dated payments to entries](#link-bills-and-dated-payments-to-entries)).
- **Actions**: Duplicate month, Reset month, Delete month
  ([Duplicate, reset or delete a month](#duplicate-reset-or-delete-a-month)).
- **Draft a summary** (Monthly Note): drafts a note for you to edit when the
  optional writing assistant is switched on; otherwise it says it is off.

Actual amounts are read-only on Month. They always come from Entries, so
fixing an actual means fixing the entry.

## Splits

Splits has an intentional **dark orange theme**, on desktop and phone, so you
always know you are working on shared money rather than your bank ledger.

### What you see

- **Group tiles**: a row under the person switcher with your named groups
  (for example a trip or home bills) and **Non-group expenses**. The group you
  have open comes first as a white tile with its balance in words, such as
  "Serene owes you $166.35" or "You owe Serene $109.50", and how many entries
  it has. The others are smaller tiles with one balance line: groups that owe
  you first, then groups you owe, then Non-group expenses ("No balance" when
  it is even), then settled groups, dimmed and marked "Settled". The cream
  tile on the left stays put: **New** creates a group and **All** opens a list
  of every group with its balance. When money is hidden every tile says
  "Balance hidden".
- **Money insights** for the selected group.
- **The group card**: a white card for the group you have open. It shows the
  group's name, its currency and how many entries it has, then the balance as
  one figure with who owes whom in words under it ("Serene owes you" in
  green, "You owe Serene" in red, or "Settled up"). Under that are a chip for
  the other person and a spending chip (select it to show or hide the
  spending-by-category chart). It reads **Spent since last settle-up** and adds
  up only what the group has spent since you last settled up, so it matches the
  entry count and the balance; a group you have never settled up reads **Group
  spend** and adds up everything. The chart behind it shows your own share of
  that same spending and says so in its middle ("Your share since last
  settle-up"), so it is half of a 50/50 chip, not a different total. The
  insights follow the same rule: what they say about "now" counts only what has
  not been settled up, and a fun fact about the whole group says "(settled or
  not, since 2 Apr)". **History** is quiet and **Settle group** is
  the one main button; on a computer **+ Add expense** sits between them. When
  money is hidden the figure is dots and the words say "Balance with Serene",
  so nothing shows who owes whom.
- **Search**, in its own field under the card, named for the group you have
  open.
- **Possible split links**: a reminder when imported bank rows may match
  splits you entered by hand ([Link a bank row to a split](#link-a-bank-row-to-a-split-you-entered-earlier)).
- **Archived batches**: groups you have already settled.
- **Activity**: the group's open expenses and settle-ups, by date. Each date
  is a quiet divider with how many entries it holds, and that day's rows sit
  together in one white block. Each row says what it was and who paid, with
  the words and amount that are yours ("you lent", "you borrowed") at the
  right.

### What each control does

- **Review matches**: compare imported bank rows with your manual splits.
  It is at the top right of the page on a computer, and the **Review
  matches** button in the reminder on a phone.
- **Settle group**: record a payment that clears the selected group.
- **Simplify settlement**: combine all open groups in the same currency into
  one amount to pay. It is at the top right of the page on a computer, and a
  row on the group card on a phone.
- **Select an expense** to edit it in place: its form replaces the row, with
  **Save**, **Cancel** and **Delete**.
- **History** (on the group card): deleted and restored split records, with
  **Restore**.

Splits is not filtered by the month picker: it always shows what is still
open.

## Ask

Ask is on the phone's bottom navigation. It is a page for adding an entry
quickly, by typing or saying it, from a receipt photo, or by filling in a
card, for finding entries by asking, such as "grab rides in september", and
for questions about your money, such as "why was August so expensive?".
On a computer it opens beside the screen you are on, from the box at the
bottom right ([Ask on a computer](#ask-on-a-computer)).

![Ask on a phone: what changed since you last looked, suggestions and the message box](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-ask.webp)

### What you see

- **Where a reply came from**: a small grey line under each reply says
  "Reply from Claude", "Reply from the basic model" or "Reply from the
  built-in rules", so you know what read your message. When Ask cannot tell
  what an entry is, it uses Other and says so; a word like "coffee" or "grab"
  goes to Food & Drinks or Taxi by itself.
- **Since you last looked**: the most useful line from Summary's money
  insights for the person you are viewing, and this month's spending against
  its plan. It shows once Summary has loaded in this visit.
- **Suggestions**: a row of buttons above the message box. Swipe it sideways
  for more.
- **The message box**: type a sentence such as "Koufu lunch $12.50, Citi",
  say it with the mic inside the box, or use the **+** inside the box for a
  receipt photo. Ask for entries the same way: "income in August", "food
  over $50 this month". A message can be up to 300 characters: a small count
  shows above the box when you get close, and a longer one is not sent.
- **New chat**: at the top right once you have said something. It clears
  the conversation and starts again.

When you are adding an entry, a **card** shows it as it will be saved: the
category, what it was, the amount (red for spending, green for money in,
plain for a transfer), the date, the wallet and whose it is. Under it are
**Date**, **Category**, **Wallet** and **Type**; **More details** adds the
posted date, the owner and a note. Tap any of them to change it, or tap the
date, wallet or person label on the card itself. What it was can be up to
500 characters and a note up to 2,000; a small count shows under the field
when you get close, and **Save** tells you if one is too long.

When Ask has to guess a detail, it says so under the card in plain words and
offers the other reading as buttons: for example "I couldn't find a wallet
called "posb", so I left the wallet out." with buttons for your real wallets.
Tap one and Ask redoes the card that way. Ask only stops to ask you a question
when two readings would save something different, such as two split groups
that both fit.

### What each control does

- **Scan receipt**, or **+** then **Take a photo** or **Choose a photo**:
  take or pick a photo of a receipt. The app reads the shop, the total and
  the date into the card. The photo is made smaller on your phone first and
  is not kept. A very large photo says so; one that is still over 1.5 MB
  after that is read on your phone instead of being sent.
- **Paste** (a suggestion, or in the **+** menu): paste a copied receipt
  photo or sentence.
- **The mic** (at the right of the message box): tap and hold the mic and
  say what you bought, then let go. Or tap it once to start and tap again
  to stop. While it listens it turns orange with a ring around it. Your words appear in the message box; check them, change
  anything that's wrong, then tap the send arrow. Nothing is sent until you
  do. The first time, your phone asks to use the microphone. If the mic
  isn't there, your browser can't take voice input; type instead.
- **Add entry** and **Add expense**: open a new card (Add expense starts
  shared on Splits).
- **Transfer** and **Income**: switch the card to a transfer between two of
  your wallets (From and To) or to money coming in.
- **Share with … on Splits**: the switch on the card shares the expense with
  the other person, as **Add to Splits** does on Entries.
- **Edit in form**: open the same entry in the usual form instead.
- **‹ Entries** (or **‹ Splits**) at the top left: after the round **+**
  or the receipt button opened Ask, go back to the page exactly as you
  left it: the same month, filters and place in the list. Your phone's
  back gesture does the same. A card you had started stays on Ask, with
  the dot on the Ask button, and is there when you come back.
- **Save**: appears beside the message box when the card is ready. It stays
  greyed out until the card has an amount and what it was.
- **Discard**: a quiet button under a card that is not saved yet; it removes the draft from the chat, and nothing was saved.
- **Back to the unsaved entry**: if you ask for entries while a card is
  still unsaved, the suggestions and the send button follow the answer. The
  card stays where it was; tap this to bring it back to the bottom with its
  own suggestions and **Save**.

Anything filled in for you is marked **Filled in from your message** or
**Read from your receipt**, with a dot on each row it filled. Check them
before you save. If the app can't read a sentence or a receipt, it says so
and you fill in the card yourself.

### Say it your way

Ask reads what you type the way a person would, including follow-ups. After
an Apple Pay purchase is saved, for example, you can type "Add to splits,
all Joyce" (or "I meant Joyce pays all of it, I paid"). Ask shows a draft:
who paid, each person's share and the amount, worked out from the entry
itself. Nothing changes until you tap **Add to Splits**; **Not now** leaves
the entry as it was, and **Undo** takes it off Splits again.

The same works for the rest of Ask: "make it $15" or "it was yesterday"
changes the card you are filling in, "Joyce paid me back $80" drafts a
settle-up payment (saved only with **Record payment**), and "move $50 from
Food to Groceries" drafts a plan change. When Ask isn't sure what you mean
(who, which card, how to split it), it asks, with buttons to tap. It never
uses an amount you didn't type, and it never saves anything by itself.

### Change things you already saved

Ask can also change what is already in the app. Each change comes back as a
draft that lists exactly what will change, before and after; nothing
changes until you tap its button, and most changes have **Undo** after.

- On a saved entry: "it was $18", "it was yesterday", "it was on my UOB
  card", "it's Joyce's", "make it Taxi", or "note: Joyce's birthday
  dinner". If a bank statement has already confirmed the entry, its amount,
  date, description and wallet stay as the bank has them, and Ask says so;
  you can still change its category, owner and note.
- Many entries at once: "make all Grab rides Transport" lists the entries it
  would move, how many there are and the first few. When it is more than
  ten, tap **Yes, change all** first. After saving, Ask offers to file new
  Grab entries the same way.
- "This was a transfer to my OCBC account" (or changing an entry's type to
  Transfer) looks for the other side, the same way the Entries transfer
  window does: the same amount in another wallet within a month. The best
  match is chosen for you, a few others are shown as buttons, and **No other
  side yet** keeps it as a transfer you pair later. If you named the account
  and nothing there matches, Ask says so and shows the others. A transfer you
  add in Ask shows the same choices before you save.
- On Splits: "new group Japan trip", "move this to Home", "settle up
  everything in Home" (a payment for the whole amount owed, saved with
  **Record payment**), "simplify everything", and "any matches in
  September?" (bank rows that may be the payment behind a split; **Match**
  links one, **Not this one** leaves it for Splits).
- Settings: "my PayNow is UOB One", "add a category Pets", "rename Taxi to
  Rides", "make Groceries green".
- Month: "note for September: Bali trip month", and "link my Netflix
  payments to the Netflix plan line".

Some things Ask won't do, so you can check them on their own screen first:
deleting entries, months or imports, importing a statement or undoing an
import, checking an account against its statement, and changing accounts
or people. Ask tells you why and opens the right page.

To understand you, Ask sends your message and the last few lines of the
conversation to the AI it uses, with the names of your people, wallets,
categories and split groups. It never sends account or card numbers, phone
numbers, or your balances and amounts from your records. When the AI isn't
available, Ask says so once and reads your message the simpler way it
always has.

### After you save

The card is marked **Saved** and the suggestions change:

- **Back to Entries** (or **Back to Splits**): go back where you came from.
- **Open this entry**: open it in Entries to edit it fully.
- **Open on Splits**: when it was shared.
- **Undo**: remove the entry (and its split) again. The card comes back so
  you can change it and save once more.

![A saved entry on Ask, with Back to Entries, Open this entry and Undo](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-ask-saved.webp)

If you usually share a purchase with your partner (the same shop, split
evenly in the same group, most of the times you bought there), Ask notices
when you add it again and asks: "You usually split this with Joyce. Add it
to Home?" **Split 50/50 in Home** turns Splits on for the new entry (it is
saved when you tap **Save**); **Not this time** puts the question away. It
only looks at your own past entries and never guesses from one purchase.

### Find entries by asking

Type what you are looking for, the way you would say it:

- "How much on public transport?" or "groceries last month"
- "Serene's shared groceries in September"
- "income in Aug", "transfers to POSB", "show my Citi card spending"
- "food over $50 this month", or "under $20", or "between $20 and $50"
- "grab rides in September" finds entries that mention Grab

Ask answers with a card: the total, how many entries it counts, and what it
looked for (the month, whose entries, the category, the wallet). The total
is the same as Entries shows for that list. **Open in Entries** opens
exactly that list; the **From Ask** chip there brings you back.

![An answer on Ask: the total for Public Transport this month, with Open in Entries](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-ask-answer.webp)

![Entries opened from Ask, with the From Ask chip above the View and scope bar](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-entries-from-ask.webp)

If what you typed could mean more than one thing (two cards both called

**Switch to …** button; nothing changes until you tap it. The total is

### Ask about your money

Ask also answers questions about your money in plain words:

- "Why was August so expensive?" or "Where did September go?": what was
  spent, against the plan and the month before, where most of it went and
  the biggest single costs.
- "How am I doing against the plan?", "Am I on track?" or "How did
  September go?": the month's spending against its plan, the same bar
  Month shows for what you earned, spent and kept, and what went furthest
  over. "Did we stick to the plan in Q3?" does the same for the three
  months of a quarter, month by month.
- "Compare August with July": both months side by side and what changed
  most.
- "What's my biggest expense this month?": the largest expenses.
- "How much income came in in August?": the income against its plan, and
  where it came from.
- "What do I owe Serene?" or "Who owes who?": what is still open between
  you on Splits.
- "How much did I invest in September?": what went into Investments and
  into your investment accounts, against what you planned to invest, and
  the entries behind it (or how much more you took out than you put in).
- "How much did we save last month?" or "Did I hit my savings target?":
  what you kept of what you earned, against your savings target.
- "Why did I spend more than I earned in August?" or "How did my spending
  compare with my income?": what you earned, spent and invested, how much
  came out of your savings (or what you kept), and where most of the
  spending went.

Questions about spending, the plan, income, investing and saving also
work for a stretch of months, such as "from October 2025 to September
2026" (up to 12 months) or "in Q3". The questions
**Ask about this** asks for the bar and Where your money goes are these same
questions.

The answer is a card with the figure you asked about (and the plan bar when
there is a plan), a few short facts, a sentence or two, and the rows behind
it. The figures are the same ones Month, Entries and Splits show. The
buttons under it open the right page: **Open in Entries** (the entries it
counted), **Open Month** or **Open Splits**. With money totals hidden, the
amounts are hidden and the sentences wait until you show them.

![A money question answered on Ask: July's spending against its plan, with Open in Entries and Open Month](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-ask-money-answer.webp)

If a payment in that month looks like a settle-up between the two of you,
or money moved between your own accounts, and it is still counted as
spending or income, Ask shows it first under **Check this first**, so the
answer isn't thrown off by it:

- **Mark as settlement** (or **Link as transfer**): link it the way Splits
  and Settings would, so it no longer counts as spending or income. Ask
  then answers with that fixed.
- **It's real spending**: leave it as it is. Ask answers with it counted
  and doesn't ask about it again for that question.

![Check this first on Ask: a PayNow from Serene to Ethan that looks like a settle-up, with Mark as settlement and It's real spending](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-ask-money-check.webp)

When Claude is set up, it may put the answer into its own words, using only
those figures, and the card is then marked **Worded by AI**; if its words
don't match the figures exactly, you see Ask's own sentence instead, with no
mark. Nothing you ask is kept once you close the tab.

Other questions, such as "should I buy a car?", aren't answered; Ask says
so.

To have a Money insights line explained, tap the line itself and choose
**Ask about this** ([Use Money insights](#use-money-insights)). On a phone Ask
opens; on a computer it opens beside the page you are on.

### Change your plan by asking

You can also change a month's plan by typing what you want:

- "Set groceries plan to $700 for November", or "plan $700 for groceries
  in November".
- "Raise groceries by $50 for November", or "cut shopping by $100 this
  month".
- "Move $50 from Shopping to Groceries in October": one line goes down, the
  other up, and what you plan to spend stays the same.
- "Add a Travel line of $300 to November".
- "Plan November like September", or "plan November like September but
  Groceries $700": September's lines are copied into November, with your
  changes on top. Lines November already has take September's amounts;
  lines only November has stay.

Ask shows a **Draft** card: each line that changes, from what it is now to
what it would be, and the month's Planned income, Planned spend and
Remaining budget before and after, the same figures Month shows for your
view. If the plan would then spend more than it expects to earn, a line
under the card says so. Nothing changes until you tap **Save plan**;
**Discard** leaves the plan as it was.

After saving, **See it on Month** opens that month, and **Undo this
change** puts the plan back. If the plan was changed somewhere else in the
meantime, Save doesn't go through and Ask tells you to ask again, so you
never overwrite a change you haven't seen.

If you don't name a month, or a word could mean two lines (say "sports"
for both Sports & Hobbies and Tennis lesson), Ask asks which, with a button
for each. A line name that isn't in the plan but is one of your categories
starts a new line; on the Household view Ask asks whose line it is.

When Claude is set up and Ask can't read a request on its own, Claude may
read it into the same draft, marked **Read by AI**; the figures are still
your plan's own, and nothing is saved until you tap Save plan.

### Ask learns what you usually do

Ask quietly notices what you choose. When you put a shop's entries in the
same category, or add them to Splits the same way, a few times, Ask
remembers it. It remembers only these small facts, such as "Bengawan Solo is
Food & Drinks" or "Hawker Dinner is split 50/50 with Joyce". It does not keep
what you type in the chat.

- **A quiet suggestion.** When you open a new entry in Ask (an Apple Pay
  purchase, or one you added), a single line can appear under it, such as
  *Food & Drinks · Add to splits 50/50 with Joyce, as you usually do*. Tap
  the tick to do it, or the cross for "not this time". It never pops up on
  its own and is never a notification.
- **Done for you.** Once Ask has seen you do the same thing three times in a
  row, it does it for a **new** Apple Pay or hand-added entry without
  asking: it sets the category, or adds the entry to Splits the usual way,
  and tells you right under the entry with an **Undo**. **Done for you**, at
  the bottom of Ask, lists the last 30 days. **Undo** puts it back and Ask
  stops trusting that habit until you repeat it. **Stop doing this** makes
  Ask forget it.
- **What it never does by itself.** Ask never changes an amount, date,
  wallet or person, never settles up, deletes or links anything, never
  touches an entry you imported or the bank has confirmed, and never touches
  an entry older than an hour. Everything else still needs your Save.

In **Settings**, **What Ask remembers** has the switch **Let Ask handle safe
things** (on to start with), lists everything Ask has learned, lets you
change or forget any of it, and has **Forget everything**.

### When Claude credits run low

Ask uses Claude. Other AI help uses Claude while the month's budget allows,
otherwise the basic model. Claude is a paid helper, and it is only used when
it is set up. When its credits run low, Ask shows a small note at the top of
the conversation:

- **Claude credits are running low**: with how much of the credit is used
  (hidden while money totals are hidden). Ask still works as usual.
- **Claude credits are used up** or **This month's Claude spending limit is
  reached**: Ask keeps working with the basic model, a simpler free reader,
  until you top up or the month resets. Check what it fills in a little more
  carefully.

On the note:

- **Top up in the Claude Console**: opens Claude's billing page in a new
  tab, where you buy more credit. The app can't buy it for you.
- **I've topped up**: after buying credit, enter how much you bought in US
  dollars and tap **Save**. The note goes away and Ask counts from the new
  amount.
- **✕**: hides the note until you close the browser tab. It comes back
  sooner only if things get worse, for example from running low to used up.

The same line, with both buttons, is in **Settings** under **Ask and AI**.
Under it, one more line says how much Claude has been used this month and
who is helping: "background help is using Claude" while the month is on
budget, "background help is using the basic model to save budget" when
Claude is running ahead of the month's pace, and "everything is using the
basic model to save budget" once nearly all of the month's budget is used.
The dollar figures are hidden while money totals are hidden. It resets at
the start of each month.

### Your conversation is kept

If you go to another page and come back to Ask, your conversation is where
you left it: what you said, the answers, a card you were filling in and
anything you had typed. It stays until you close the browser tab, or tap
**New chat**. While a conversation is waiting for you, the Ask button at
the bottom shows a small orange dot. Receipt photos are not kept: the card
keeps what was read from the photo, but the photo itself goes when you
leave Ask.

## Imports

### What you see

- **Steps**: **1 · Bring files**, **2 · Review**, **3 · Done**. A finished
  step shrinks to one line, and while a file is in review Bring files
  shrinks too. While you work through files, the steps stay at
  the top of the screen on a computer or tablet (on a phone they scroll with
  the page). Once you scroll down they stretch across the window as one
  short line, with a soft shadow under them. Two more rows sit under the
  steps:
  - **Your files:** one chip per file you brought this time, in the order to
    review them. A green chip with a tick is done (a finished step looks the
    same), the outlined one with a file icon says **reviewing**, and one
    with a clock says **waiting**. Point at a chip to see the full file
    name. **File 2 of 4** says where you are. Select a waiting file to open it; if
    you changed rows or approved fixes in the file in review, the app asks
    first, because opening another file drops those changes. A file you roll
    back leaves the chips.
  - **Before you can commit:** what still needs you in the file in review,
    in the order to fix it: accounts to map, cards that don't match the
    statement (with the amount when money totals are shown), rows to check.
    A card whose earlier statement was never imported says which one to
    import first instead, for example "OCBC 365 Credit Card: import Sept 2026
    first".
    A card that has no account yet is counted once, under **Map 1 account**.
    Select one to go straight to it. A green chip counts the rows already
    matched to your entries.
- **Import and certify**: comes first. Drop or select your files here. A
  statement PDF or a bank export goes straight to Review; a plain CSV first
  asks you to match its columns. Once files are waiting it shrinks to one
  line, **Drop more files, or Select file**, so your files are on the first
  screen. Beside it, **Waiting for review** says how many files you added;
  select it to go to the queue.
- **File intake queue**: the files you dropped, in the order to review them.
- **Import inbox**: your bank run, planned for you. It shows one line,
  **Still missing: 3 of 13**, and opens with **Show**. It starts open when
  nothing is waiting, and folds away once files are queued or one is in
  review. Each file you queue that matches a file on the list is ticked off
  and lowers the count. Open it to see bank sessions to open, needed files,
  accounts that are current and split cleanup. Each bank is one row with
  how many of its files are still missing; open a bank for its steps, an
  **Open portal** link and each file you still need (for example the OCBC
  365 Card statement for Aug 2026). Files are grouped by owner (Tim, Joyce,
  Shared), and each account is one box with its download step, listing
  every month it still needs. A statement that
  covers several cards is asked for once ("covers UOB Lady's Card and UOB
  One Card").
- **Review order**: statements are reviewed oldest month first; optional
  activity files come after.
- **Recent imports**: every import batch, with its kind (PDF statement,
  Mid-cycle, CSV import or Manual), dates, account, status and a rollback
  button. It starts closed; select it to open the list.

![Import and certify: drop your files; once some are waiting it is one line, "Drop more files, or Select file", with Paste rows or set defaults under it](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/imports-upload.webp)

### What each control does

- **Drop a CSV, PDF, or XLS here**: drop or select one file. Drop several at
  once and they wait in the **File intake queue**, in the order to review
  them: statements first (older months first), then activity. An activity
  file says how many of its rows a statement in the queue already covers.
  Select **Review** on each. You can also drop files anywhere on the Imports
  page; it says **Drop files anywhere on this page** while you drag. A file
  dropped on the file you are reviewing is not added. A single file dropped
  while you review another opens straight away; the other waits in the
  queue again. **Review** asks first if you changed the file in review.
- **How importing works**: a folded row under the drop zone with the notes
  about PDF and XLS files, trustworthy statements and rollback.
- **Paste rows or set defaults**: opens the fields below. It opens by
  itself when a plain CSV needs them. Once files are waiting it also holds
  those notes.
  - **Source label** and **Batch note**: optional names so you recognise
    the import later. A plain CSV is named by its file unless you typed a
    source label.
  - **Default account** and **Default owner**: used for rows that do not
    say which account or person they belong to. Choose the account before
    dropping a card activity file.
  - **CSV content**: paste CSV rows directly.
- **Show** / **Hide** (on the Import inbox): opens or folds the bank list.
- **Use with import form** and **Not available** (on a file in the Import
  inbox): point at the file, or tap it on a phone, to see them. **Use with
  import form** fills the defaults (account, owner, source label and note)
  from that file; **Not available** sets the file aside for a few days when
  the bank has not published it yet.
- **Change** (on the Bring files step): opens the files again while one is
  in review.
- **Preview import**, **Commit import to ledger**, **Start over**: see
  [Import a bank statement](#import-a-bank-statement). The commit button
  stays in view at the bottom of the review, with what the commit will do.
  While something still needs you it says how many things are left;
  **Jump to next** takes you to the first one. On a computer, press **J**
  for the next thing to fix and **K** for the previous one (not while you
  are typing in a field).
- **Commit and open next**: while another file waits, the commit button
  says so (**Save statement balance and open next** for a statement whose
  rows are all in already, **Certify and open next** for one whose rows all
  match entries you have). After the commit the next file opens straight
  away, with a short summary of the file you just committed above it and
  its **Roll back this import**. The last file's button keeps its usual words,
  and the full **Done** step shows when it is committed.
- **Skip for now**: while another file waits, moves the file in review to
  the end of your files and opens the next one. Reopen it from its chip
  whenever you like; you don't need the file again. If you changed rows or
  approved fixes, the app asks first, because skipping drops those changes.
- **Remove from** *card* (on a card in the review): appears when you bring a
  card's activity file and an Apple Pay entry you saved on that card is not
  in the file, while another card's bank rows show the same purchase (the
  same shop and amount, within a couple of days). You most likely paid with
  the other card, so the purchase is counted twice. The card names each entry
  and the other card's row that matches it. Select **Remove from** *card*,
  then confirm in the window: the entries come off that card when you commit,
  and the purchase stays on the other card. **Keep as is** leaves them, and
  **Undo** on the card takes your choice back before you commit. Rolling the
  import back brings the entries back. When the app is less sure (the dates
  are three days apart, or the entry is part of a split or a plan) it only
  lists the entry under **Also check** and never offers to remove it. The
  app never removes an entry on its own.
- **Rollback import** on a batch: undo it ([Undo an import](#undo-an-import)).

# How do I…

Step-by-step recipes. Each assumes you start from the screen named in step 1.

## Add an expense

1. Open **Entries** and choose the month at the top right.
2. Select the round **+** button. It opens a new entry card on
   [Ask](#ask) (beside the page on a wide screen): type the amount and what
   it was (or say it in the message box, or use a receipt photo), check the
   rows under the card and select **Save**. To use the full form instead,
   select **Edit in form**.
3. In the form, choose the **Category**, **Date**, **Wallet** (the account or
   card you paid with), **Owner** (who paid) and **Type** (Expense).
4. Type the **Amount** and a **Description**. Use the merchant name, such as
   `FairPrice` or `Grab`, so a later bank import can recognise it.
5. Add a **Note** if the reason matters later.
6. To share it straight away, open **Add this expense to Splits** at the
   bottom of the form.
7. Select **Save**. The entry appears under its day and the totals update.

![Adding an entry on Entries](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/entries-add.webp)

![On a phone, the + button opens a new entry card on Ask](/faq/guide/phone/thumbs/phone-entries-add.webp)

Manual entries are marked **Manual provisional** until a bank file confirms
them ([Record entries before the bank file arrives](#record-entries-before-the-bank-file-arrives)).

## Edit or delete an entry

1. Open **Entries** and find the entry ([Find an entry](#find-an-entry-filters-search-and-months)).
2. Select the row. It opens as a card with rows for each field, in place
   (a sheet on a phone).
3. Type over what it was or the amount on the card, change the date, wallet
   or owner on the card's small labels, or change a row such as
   **Category** or **Note** (on a phone the posted date and type are under
   **More details**, with the date and wallet). Select **Save**, or **Cancel**
   to leave it as it was.
4. To remove it, select **Delete entry**, then confirm.

Good to know:

- If the entry is shared, changing its amount, date, description or owner
  updates the split too, keeping each person's percentage. Changing the note
  or category asks whether to update the split as well.
- On an entry a bank statement has confirmed, the bank details (date,
  description, wallet, amount) are locked, and so is whether the money came
  in or went out. You can still change the category, note, owner and splits,
  and switch the type between Income and Transfer in, or Expense and
  Transfer out
  ([I can't change a statement-confirmed entry](#i-cant-change-a-statement-confirmed-entry)).
- If the entry is part of a settled split, some changes are refused until you
  undo the settle-up ([I can't change a split](#i-cant-change-a-split)).
- Save needs an amount above zero. If the amount is empty or isn't a number,
  the entry stays open with your changes so you can fix it.

## Plan a month

1. Open **Summary**, select your name at the top left (Household is
   read-only) and pick the month ([Pick a month](#pick-a-month)).
2. Under **Income**, select **+ Add income source** and enter each expected
   income.
3. Under **Bills and dated payments**, select **+ Add bill or dated payment** for each fixed
   commitment: savings, loan, insurance, subscriptions. Give each a
   category, date, name, planned amount and optionally an account.
   ![A new bill or dated payment opens as an editable row](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/month-add-planned.webp)
4. Under **Monthly budget**, select **+ Add monthly budget** for flexible
   spending such as Food, Groceries, Transport and Shopping.
5. Select **Save** on each row.
6. Check the top of the page: it should not say **Planned more than you earn**,
   and the **Saved** target should be what you want to put aside.

> Tip: keep monthly budgets broad. One Food budget works better than a row for
> every restaurant. Plan only what is stable as bills and dated payments.

Next month, use **Actions → Duplicate month** to copy the plan and adjust it,
or type it on Ask: "plan November like October but Groceries $700"
([Change your plan by asking](#change-your-plan-by-asking)).

### See where every planned dollar goes

In Plan view, **Plan Studio** shows your whole month plan by where your money goes: what
comes in, **Spend**, **Save**, **Invest** and what is **left over**. The big
picture across the top is as wide as the page; under it, how the month is
going and **What if I save more?** sit side by side (stacked on a phone).

- **The bar** is your planned money in. Drag a line to move money from one
  job to the next (the arrow keys work too). Tap a job's name under the bar to
  see and resize its categories. If the plan puts out more than comes in, the
  bar goes past the dashed **Planned in** line and the top says **The plan
  doesn't add up**, with a button that fixes it in one tap.
- **One list, grouped by what the money does.** **Money in**, **Spend**,
  **Save** and **Invest**. Your bills with a due date sit in **Spend** with the
  budgets, with a small tag such as *due 2 Oct*, earliest first.
- **Every line has a slider.** Drag it, or use the **−** and **+** buttons for
  one step. For a finer amount, hold **Shift** while you drag (on a phone,
  press and hold, then drag). The lock keeps a line from changing.
- **A small card tells you about the line** while you hover or focus its
  slider (on a phone, tap the **i** button) and while you drag: what you
  planned and spent last month, the average of the last three months, what you
  have spent so far and what is left, whether you are ahead of or behind where
  the line usually is today, and what your change does to **Left over**
  (for example, *+$40 → Left over $310*). It shows nothing while your money is
  hidden. When you pin the details open, tapping outside closes them without
  changing the slider underneath. The next tap can change the plan.
- **To type an exact amount, tap the amount.** A small box opens: type
  something like 137 or 137.50 and press Enter (Esc cancels). It shows the
  change from what is saved, and has quick **+$5, +$10, +$50** buttons.
- **Take from** says where the money for a change comes from. Left over by

- **What if I save more?** Slide or type a bigger savings target to see where
  the money would come from, then **Apply to plan** or **Cancel**. It only
  changes the plan; it does not guess what you will spend.
- **Planned in** under the bar is split by where the money comes from (each
  salary, bonus or other line).
- **Money that came in but is not in the plan.** If more arrived than you
  planned, a quiet line says so, for example *You got $500 that isn't in the
  plan*. **Add to Savings** asks first (it says how much); then your Savings and the
  money you planned to come in both go up by that amount, so the plan still
  adds up. **Cancel** changes nothing. It does not guess what is still to
  come.
- Nothing is kept until you tap **Save plan**. **Undo** steps back,
  **Reset to saved** drops every change. If saving fails your changes stay.
- **A line's ⋯ menu** is where you edit its note, change its details (name,
  category, date, account), match it to entries (for a line with a due date) or
  remove it (it asks first). **Add a line** (in Spend) and **Add income** add
  new ones; a new line is Spend unless its category is Investments, and it can
  have an optional due date.
- **Sliders | List.** The switch beside Undo and Discard changes (or Save plan
  on a wide screen) changes how the plan shows. On a phone it sits in the
  floating View and scope bar when there is room. **List** is the plan as a
  list, where you can see and sort everything. Changes you have not saved
  with the sliders are kept while you look at the list (a dot on Sliders says

## Link bills and dated payments to entries

A monthly budget's actual comes from every entry in its category. A bill or
dated payment's actual comes only from the entries you link, because several of
them can share a category (for example Bills).

1. On **Summary**, pick the month in your person view.
2. In **Bills and dated payments**, select **Link entries** under the item's actual.
3. The list starts narrowed to likely matches. Use the chips **Linked**,
   **Same category**, **Same account** and **This month only**, or type in
   **Description filter**, to narrow it further.
4. Tick the entries that pay for this item.
5. Select **Save matches**. The actual and variance update.

![Match bill or dated payment: tick the entries that pay for it](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/month-link-entries.webp)

The app remembers what you linked so it can suggest the same kind of entries
next month. It never links them without you.

## Duplicate, reset or delete a month

On **Summary**, pick the month, then select **Actions**.

![The Actions menu on Month](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/month-actions.webp)

- **Duplicate month** copies every plan row of this month into the next month
  and opens it. If the next month already has a plan, it just opens it and
  changes nothing.
- **Reset month** clears this month's plan rows, entries and totals. Type
  `reset month` and select **Confirm reset month**.
- **Delete month** removes the month entirely, including its summary

## Import a bank statement

1. Open **Imports**. The **Import inbox** lists the files you need, grouped
   by bank. Select **Open portal** to sign in to your bank, and download the
   listed files ([Which file do I download?](#which-file-do-i-download-from-my-bank)).
   No renaming needed.
2. Under **Import and certify**, select **Paste rows or set defaults** and
   choose the **Default account** the file belongs to (for a card activity
   export this matters most).
3. Drop the file on **Drop a CSV, PDF, or XLS here**, or select it. PDFs and
   bank exports turn straight into rows; a plain CSV first asks you to match
   its columns (**Data mapping**), then **Preview import**.
4. If the file names a card or account the app does not know yet (for example
   "Detected: UOB One Card"), choose which of your accounts it is under
   **Unknown accounts need mapping before commit**, or select **Create
   account**. **Commit import to ledger** stays disabled until every account
   is chosen.
   ![The file names a card the app does not know yet: choose the account](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/imports-mapping.webp)
5. Review the preview ([Read the import preview](#read-the-import-preview)).
   Fix anything under **Exceptions to resolve**. The chips under
   **Before you can commit:** at the top, or **Jump to next** beside the
   commit button, take you to each thing still to fix.
6. Select **Commit import to ledger**. The rows appear on Entries, and the
   batch appears under **Recent imports**. The **Done** step (a teal box)
   names the file, the accounts it went into and the statement period, and
   says **No further action needed** when every card balanced. It shows what
   changed on each card (rows added, entries confirmed or moved, entries left
   for the next statement), with **Import another file** at the top
   (**Close** while another file waits) and **Roll back this import** at the
   bottom. **Recent imports** says **File imported:** with the file's name,
   and lists the five imports before it, so you can check them against your
   files. When more files are queued the button reads **Commit and open
   next**; the next file opens after the commit under a short summary of
   the one you committed.

![A bank export turned into preview rows, ready to commit](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/imports-preview.webp)

![After committing, the batch appears under Recent imports](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/imports-committed.webp)

If the import may match splits you entered by hand, the app offers **Review
split matches** (or **Later**).

Select **Start over** at any time to clear the draft. Your original file is
never stored; only the rows you commit are saved.

## Import an investment statement (iFAST and StashAway)

An iFAST monthly statement (the "Wrap" PDF) does two things when you import
it: it records the money you put in, and it saves what the statement says the
account was worth on its last day.

1. **Import the bank statement first.** Your bank's own row for the payment
   (for OCBC, "IBG GIRO … IFAST FINANCIAL PL-" with your iFAST account
   number) should already be in the app. Then the import can see it.
2. On **Imports**, drop the iFAST PDF. Download it from iFAST as the whole
   monthly statement; a file with its pages cut off is refused and says so.
3. If the app has no account for it yet, the review says the account is
   unknown. Select **Create account**: the name is filled in from the
   statement (for example "Joyce iFAST"), the type is **Investment**, and you
   only choose the owner. The next statement finds the account by the last
   four digits of its number, so you will not be asked again.
4. Read the **Investment statement** box. It says what the account was worth
   on the statement's last day, and for each amount you put in whether it is
   the same money as a bank payment already in the app ("$500.00 put in on 8
   Jul 2026 is the OCBC 360 payment of 3 Jul 2026. They are saved as one
   transfer, not two."). If there is no matching bank payment it says so and
   saves the amount on its own; you can link the two later in Entries.
5. Select **Commit import to ledger** (**Save statement value** for a month
   when you put nothing in). The **Done** box names the account and what was
   saved, for example "Saved Tim StashAway's value: $15,357.71 on 31 Mar 2026"
   (the amount stays hidden while money is hidden), and, when you put money in,
   how many payments were added and paired with bank transfers. **Recent
   imports** lists the statement under the same account and date, so its **By
   account** filter finds it, and **Roll back this import** removes the saved
   value and any payments.

What it does and does not do:

- A payment you made into iFAST shows once, as a move of money into your
  investments. The bank's payment and iFAST's record of it become one
  transfer, so the same $500 is not counted twice.
- Switching between funds inside iFAST, buying units each month and dividends
  that iFAST reinvests are not money moving to or from your bank, so they do
  not appear in your entries.
- **What it was worth** is the statement's own figure for that day (the
  market value of everything in the account plus any cash in iFAST). It is
  kept for reference. It is not a bank balance, it is never checked against
  your entries, and it is not a prediction. It appears on **Summary** under
  **The accounts shown**, with the statement's date.
- Importing the same statement again adds nothing. A corrected statement for
  the same month replaces the figure saved for that month, and says so before
  you save.
- If you roll the import back ([Undo an import](#undo-an-import)), the amount
  you put in is removed, the bank's row goes back to how it was, and the
  worth it saved is removed (or put back to the earlier statement's).
- Only Singapore dollar statements are read. Another currency is refused with
  its name.

### StashAway statements

A StashAway monthly "Statement of Account" PDF is imported the same way, in
any order and without importing a bank statement first. Drop the whole PDF on
**Imports**; if the app has no account for it yet, select **Create account**
(the name is filled in from the statement's owner, so you may want to rename
it, for example "Tim StashAway"; the type is **Investment** and the
institution **StashAway**). The **Investment statement** box then says what
the portfolio was worth on the statement's last day: StashAway's closing
balance in Singapore dollars, after the month's gains or losses and its fee,
which are inside the portfolio and not money moving to or from your bank. That
figure is saved for reference and shown on **Summary** with its date, like an
iFAST one. Most months you put nothing in, so **Save statement value** is the
only step. If you did deposit or withdraw, the amount is saved as money moved
into or out of your investments and matched with your bank's payment when it
is already in the app; when the statement lists only the month's net amount,
the entry is dated the last day of the month and the review says so. If a
statement starts at a different amount from where the earlier one ended, the
box tells you a statement may be missing, and you can still save it. A
statement in another currency, or with its first page cut off, is refused
and says why.

## Which file do I download from my bank?

The Import inbox names each file. In general, download statement PDFs first
(oldest month first), then an activity export for the current month.

### UOB

- **Statements**: the monthly PDF for each card or account (credit cards,
  including one PDF with two cards such as UOB One Card plus UOB Privi Miles,
  and UOB One savings).
- **During the month**: the "current transactions" history as `.xls`, for a
  card or an account.

### OCBC

- **Statements**: OCBC 365 and OCBC Infinity Cashback card PDFs, OCBC 360
  account PDFs, and Child Development Account (CDA) PDFs.
- **During the month**: the transaction history `.csv` for a card or the 360
  account. Choose the OCBC account as the Default account first.

OCBC 360 activity has a transaction date and a value date. The app uses the
value date for the balance check, so a transfer on 31 May that clears on 2
June belongs to June's statement.

### Citibank

- **Statements**: the credit-card PDF (Citi Rewards and Citibank Miles
  layouts).
- **During the month**: the card activity `.csv`. Choose the Citibank card as
  the Default account before dropping it; the file has no headings, so the
  account tells the app how to read it.

### iFAST (investments)

- **Statements**: the monthly "Wrap" statement PDF for each iFAST account,
  the whole file ([Import an investment statement](#import-an-investment-statement-ifast-and-stashaway)).
  Each account has its own number and its own statements; import each.

### StashAway (investments)

- **Statements**: the monthly "Statement of Account" PDF, the whole file
  ([StashAway statements](#stashaway-statements)). Import each month you have.

### HSBC

- **Statements**: the HSBC Visa Revolution PDF. These are scanned images, so
  the app reads them with text recognition inside your browser (nothing is
  uploaded). It takes a little longer; check the rows against the PDF before
  committing, because scanned text can be misread.
- **Amount from statement total**: when the scan can't read one amount but
  the statement's total says exactly what it must be, the app fills it in
  and marks that row "Amount from statement total". Check it against the
  PDF; the mark goes once you change the amount.
- **When the rows don't add up**: if the app can't read every row, the review
  opens anyway with the rows it found and a card showing the statement's
  purchases, the rows found and the difference. It offers **Add row** for a
  missing row only when an amount it read matches the difference exactly, and
  asks you to type an amount it couldn't read. You can also correct a misread
  amount under **Edit rows**. Nothing is saved, and you can't commit, until
  the rows add up to the statement.

### Any other bank

- Download a `.csv` export, paste or drop it, and match its columns under
  **Data mapping**. It can use one signed amount column or separate expense
  and income columns.
- A PDF no reader recognises can be offered to the optional writing assistant
  only if you tick the box to allow it. Its rows always start as **Needs
  review** and never save a statement balance.

## Read the import preview

The preview shows what will happen before anything is saved.

A statement PDF is reviewed card by card. Under the statement's name, date
and period, each card has its own section with an outcome:

- **Resolved**: the card matches the statement. The section shrinks to one
  line.
- **Fix ready**: a suggested fix would make it match
  ([Fix a statement that does not balance](#fix-a-statement-that-does-not-balance)).
- **Partially resolved**: the fixes leave some of the difference.
- **Earlier statement missing**: a statement between the last one you saved
  and this one was never imported. **Also check** names it; import it first.
- **Needs manual review**: the app found causes it can't fix for you, listed
  under **Also check**.
- **Still mismatched**: nothing explains the difference yet.

Each card's name starts with whose account the statement goes to, in that
person's colour (the same colour as their rows on Entries), and ends with the
card's last four digits when the statement prints them, for example
"Joyce OCBC 365 Credit Card • Oct 2026 • ending 7528". If that is the wrong
account, use **Change card accounts**. After the first statement is saved, the
account remembers those four digits, so the next statement finds it even if
the bank renames the card.

Under its name a card shows the statement's balance and your ledger's
balance once this import is saved, for example "Statement: $33.15 owed •
Ledger after import: $119.26 in credit • difference $152.41". Under that it
counts its rows, for example "34 new rows · none already in your ledger".
New rows become new entries. Rows already in your ledger are entries you
added before, from an activity export or by hand, that this statement
confirms, or rows an earlier import of this statement saved. They are not
added twice.

A card statement that asks for money also says what to pay, for example
"Pay $33.15 by 23 Oct 2026 · minimum $33.15", or "Pay $264.34 now" when the
bank printed IMMEDIATE because the card is past due. The due date and
minimum are saved with the statement for **Money insights**.

When the statement has the bank's own charges, a brick-red box above the checks
lists them, for example "Bank charges on this statement: Late charge $100.00
· Interest $4.37". It also says when the bank reversed one ("Reversed by
the bank: Annual fee $196.20") and repeats the bank's notices, such as "The
statement says this card is past due" or the penalty interest rate. A card
with charges opens even when it balances. Late charges, interest, annual
fees and currency fees are filed under **Fees**; a reversal is filed under
Fees too and takes the charge back out of it.

Each card lists its rows read-only (**Rows on this card's statement**) and,
when it does not balance, **How the difference adds up**. **Edit rows** opens
the full row editor; **Edit statement details** the statement's dates and
balance; **Change card accounts** which account each card goes to; **More
checks** the remaining counts and checks.

For every import:

- **Counts**: how many rows will import, how many existing entries will be
  confirmed, how many are **already covered** (duplicates) and how many
  **need review**.
- **Exceptions to resolve**: the only things you must act on, such as an
  unknown account, an unknown category, a row decision or a statement that
  does not balance.
- **Statement account mapping** (PDFs): match each account found in the
  statement to one of yours, or select **Create account**. The new account's
  opening balance is filled in from the statement.
- **Statement checkpoint** (PDFs): the statement's dates and ending balance,
  saved when you commit. For a credit card, enter the amount owed as a
  positive number, as printed by the bank.
- **Rows**: each row can be **Exclude row** or **Include row**. A row marked
  **Matched to ledger** will confirm an entry you already have instead of
  adding a duplicate. **View match** compares the two side by side.
- **Already covered rows**: duplicates the app skipped. They stay visible; use
  **Include row** only if the match was wrong.
- **Activity inside a confirmed statement**: a statement you committed is the
  complete record for its dates. An activity row dated inside it is skipped
  when the statement has it, even worded differently ("Already on the
  confirmed May 2026 statement as …"), and left out with a reason when the
  statement doesn't. Include it only if the statement is wrong. Rows after
  the statement import as usual.

A statement preview shows **Matched** for each account when its balance
agrees with the bank. When every row on the statement is already in your
ledger and the balance matches, the bar above the button says so, for
example "All 31 rows on this statement are already in your ledger and the
balance matches":

- If some of those rows came from an activity export (CSV or XLS) and are
  not certified yet, the button reads **Certify 31 matched entries**
  (**Certify and open next** while another file waits). Saving certifies
  them against the statement; nothing new is added.
- If the rows came in earlier (for example from activity exports) but this
  statement was never saved, the button reads **Save statement balance**
  (**Save statement balance and open next** while another file waits). It
  adds no entries; it saves each card's closing balance and dates, which
  marks the month as confirmed by the bank.
- If you imported this statement before, each card says **Already imported
  on** the date and the file it came from. Saving it again would only add a
  second import with no new rows, so **Start over** is the main button and
  **Save it again anyway** asks first.

**Save empty statement checkpoint** only appears for a statement with no
transactions at all.

## Fix a statement that does not balance

The statement check looks for the reason first and suggests a fix, using the
actual rows and amounts. The most common one: a purchase you recorded on one
card that the statement lists under another card on the same statement. It
shows, for example, **2 entries belong on UOB One Card**, lists each entry
with the date you bought it, the date the bank posted it and the amount, and
says how much of the difference the move takes away.

- Select **Move 2 entries to UOB One Card** (the orange button; **Keep as is**
  beside it leaves things alone). A box asks you to confirm, because both
  cards' totals change. Choose **Move entries** to approve it, or **Cancel**
  to change nothing. The check updates straight away; nothing is saved until
  you commit, and **Undo** takes the approval back. The statement then
  confirms the moved entries instead of adding copies.
- An entry marked **Check this one** is a less certain match: approve it on
  its own with **Move it** after you have looked at it.
- **Do this for me next time when the statement proves it**: from then on,
  in this browser, fixes the statement proves (one clear match, and the
  fixes make every card match) are applied as the preview opens. A notice
  says so, each fix keeps its **Undo**, nothing is saved until you commit,
  and **Ask me first next time** turns it off.
- **Keep as is** hides the suggestion.
- An entry dated just before the statement closed, with no posted date yet,
  may be suggested for **the next statement**.
- **Also check** lists what the app found but can't fix for you, such as a
  second copy of a purchase or an entry that isn't on the statement.
- When the balance before the statement doesn't match and a month's statement
  was never imported, **Also check** names it, for example "Your Sept 2026
  statement isn't imported yet. Your ledger stops at the 1 Aug 2026 statement
  ($111.93 owed), and this statement starts from $264.34 owed." Select
  **Start over**, import that statement first, then this one.
- Entries dated after the statement closed are listed as staying provisional
  for the next one. They are not part of the difference.

If you commit while a card still says **Mismatch**, the app asks first. It
also asks you to refresh the check if you changed rows after it ran.

When a statement says **Mismatch**, the preview also shows five boxes: **Before
statement period**, **Already in ledger during this period**, **PDF activity
in this preview**, **Rows the PDF can remove** and **Projected ledger after
preview**. Hover or focus a box to see exactly which dates and rows it uses.

1. Open **Already in ledger during this period** first. It lists entries
   already saved in that statement's dates that the PDF did not confirm.
2. For each row, select **Open** to see the entry in a new tab and compare it
   with the PDF:
   - In the PDF but not matched: check its date and amount on the entry.
   - Not in the PDF and not real: select **Delete** (or **Delete all** if the
     whole list is wrong).
   - Real, but the bank posted it after the statement closed: select **Set
     posted date** if your bank app shows the posted date, or **Defer** to
     move it to the next statement.
   - On the wrong card: use the suggested move above, or change its wallet.
3. Check **PDF rows not included yet**: include any real statement row you
   excluded.
4. The check refreshes as you go. Commit once it says **Matched**.

If the listed rows add up to exactly the difference, fixing them will balance
the statement. Delete is only for rows that are missing from the bank, are
duplicates, or belong to another account.

## Undo an import

1. Open **Imports**, open **Recent imports** and find the batch (use **By
   account** to narrow the list).
2. Select **Rollback import** on the batch's row.
3. Check the name in the sheet that opens (a dialog on a computer) and
   select **Confirm rollback** (or **Cancel**).

![Rolling back an import asks you to confirm](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/imports-rollback.webp)

What happens:

- Rows the import added are removed. Entries you had typed by hand that the
  import had matched go back to how you typed them, keeping later changes
  such as category, note or splits.
- Entries a statement moved to another card go back to the card they were on.
- Activity exports can always be rolled back.
- A PDF statement can be rolled back while it is the newest statement for
  that account. Older statements show **Statement locked**, because later
  statements depend on them. Roll back the newest first. A statement that
  moved entries off another card is also locked once that card has a newer
  statement.
- A shared entry whose split is already settled can block a rollback until
  you undo that settle-up.

Rolling back is all or nothing, and a batch cannot be rolled back twice.

This example uses a sample two-card statement (two credit cards on one PDF)
and activity exports that grow during the month.

1. **Import the first statement.** Map each card in the PDF to its account and
   check that both cards say Resolved before committing. The app remembers
   each card's last four digits, so the next statement maps its cards by
   itself.
   ![First statement: two cards mapped and both resolved](/faq/guide/examples/thumbs/01-jan-two-card-pdf-mapped-and-matched.webp)
2. **Save proof without duplicates.** If you review the same statement again,

   only.
   ![All rows already covered: save the statement checkpoints only](/faq/guide/examples/thumbs/02-jan-two-card-pdf-all-duplicates-save-checkpoints.webp)
3. **Import activity during the month.** Each new export repeats earlier rows;
   the preview skips those and keeps only the new ones. An export also lists
   pending charges. If a later export no longer lists an entry from an
   earlier one (usually a hold the bank released), the preview names it and
   importing removes it. Tick **Keep this entry** to leave it, or roll the
   import back to bring it back.
   ![A growing activity export: old rows skipped, new rows kept](/faq/guide/examples/thumbs/04-midcycle-snapshot-2.webp)
4. **Import the next statement.** Rows you already have are confirmed in
   place, keeping your categories, notes and splits. Only rows missing from

   ![Next statement confirms earlier rows and adds one late row](/faq/guide/examples/thumbs/07-feb-two-card-pdf-duplicates-plus-late-row-matched.webp)
5. **If you exclude a real row by mistake**, only that card needs a manual
   review, and **Also check** names the row. Include it again and both cards

## Reconcile and close a statement period

Reconciling means proving your ledger matches the bank's closing balance.

The easy way is to import the statement PDF: when it says **Matched** and you
commit, the statement balance is saved and every row it confirmed becomes
**Statement certified**.

To save a balance by hand (for example, a bank the app cannot read):

1. Open **Settings → Accounts**.
2. Select **Reconcile** on the account.
3. Choose the **Statement month** and check the **Statement ending balance**
   (it starts with the app's current balance; type the bank's figure).
   Fill in **Statement start date** and **Statement end date** only for card
   cycles that do not follow the calendar month.
4. Select **Save checkpoint** (it becomes available once a month is chosen).

![Reconcile: save a statement closing balance for an account](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/settings-reconcile.webp)

The account then says "Reconciled to <month> statement", or how far off it
is. If it is off, use **Compare statement** to upload the PDF and see which
rows match, which are missing and which are extra, without importing
anything. Missing rows can be added and wrong directions fixed from there.
An entry you recorded yourself counts as a match even if you used your own
words, or the bank posted it a few days after you bought it.

When the PDF holds more than one card, the comparison looks at all of them.
Under **Suggested fixes** it can offer to:

- **Move** entries to the card the statement lists them under, for example a
  purchase you recorded on Lady's Card that the bank put on One Card.
- **Remove the copy** of a purchase that is already on another card from the
  statement, when you had recorded it by hand as well.

Nothing changes until you select the fix, and **Undo** puts the entries back
exactly as they were. A fix that would put another saved statement out of
balance is not offered; it is listed under **Also check** instead.

After a period closes:

1. Pair any transfers under **Settings → Unresolved transfers**.
2. Link any manual splits to their bank rows ([Link a bank row to a split](#link-a-bank-row-to-a-split-you-entered-earlier)).
3. Tidy categories and owners.
4. Leave the import in Recent imports, so it can be rolled back if needed.

## Record entries before the bank file arrives

You can keep the month current by hand (or with Apple Pay) and let the bank
confirm it later.

1. Add each purchase with the real merchant name, account, amount and date.
2. When the activity export arrives, import it. Rows that match your entries
   show as matches and confirm your entries instead of adding copies.
3. Keep truly new rows included. Leave exact and probable duplicates skipped.
   Check near matches yourself.
4. When the statement arrives, import it. Matching entries become
   **Statement certified**, keeping your category, owner, note and splits.

If the bank describes a purchase very differently, it may show as a near
match or not match at all. Compare account, amount and date before deciding.

## Split an expense

There are two ways, depending on where the expense is.

### From Entries (the bank row is already there)

1. Open **Entries** and select the entry.
2. Select **Add to splits**.
3. Pick the group from **Split group**. The split is created as soon as you
   choose; **Cancel** closes the box without sharing anything.
4. The split starts 50/50 with the entry's owner as the payer. Select
   **View split** to change the people, shares, group, category or note.

![Add to splits asks which group the entry belongs to](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/entries-add-to-splits.webp)

### From Splits (no bank row yet, or cash)

1. Open **Splits** and select the group.
2. Select **+ Add expense**. The new expense opens as a card: the group, the
   date, who paid, the shares (50 / 50 to start) and how it was paid.
3. Type what it was and the total on the card. The total is in the group's
   currency.
4. Each person's share is a row. Select one to set the share as a
   percentage or an exact amount; the other person gets the rest. When a
   total does not divide evenly, choose who gets the odd cent.
5. Check **Category** and **Group**. The date, who paid, how it was paid,
   the currency, the evidence and a note are in the rows below them (under
   **More details** on a phone).
6. Select **Save expense**.

![New split expense: the card with the total, then a row for each share, the category and group](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-add-expense.webp)

A split made here is not linked to a bank row yet. When the bank row arrives,
link it instead of adding it again ([Link a bank row to a split](#link-a-bank-row-to-a-split-you-entered-earlier)).

To change a split, select it in the activity list (or Tab to it and press
Enter or Space). It opens as the same card you used to add it: on a phone as a
sheet, on a wide screen in the list in place of the row. Change the total, a share, the category or the group, open
**More details** on a phone for the rest (on a wide screen, change the date, who paid or how it was paid from the chips under the total), and select **Save expense**.
**Delete** is at the bottom left and asks before it removes anything: select
**Delete**, then **Delete split row**. Escape or **Cancel** closes the card
without saving, and the cursor goes back to the split you opened. A deleted
split can be restored ([Restore a deleted split](#restore-a-deleted-split)).

A split that has been settled up (or included in a simplified settlement)
opens read-only: you can read it, but not change it. A line at the top says
when it was settled ("Settled on 17 May 2026") or simplified, and the button
under it (**Undo settle-up** or **Undo simplification**) opens that activity
again. After the
undo the same card turns editable and you can change and save it.

![Selecting a split turns its row into a card with the total, a row for each share, the category and group](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-edit.webp)

![Deleting a split asks you to confirm](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-delete.webp)

## Link a bank row to a split you entered earlier

1. Open **Splits**. When bank rows may match splits you entered by hand, a
   banner says "possible split links".
2. Select **Review matches**.
3. Each white card has the **Existing split** and the **Imported ledger row**
   as two rows, a chip for how likely the match is, and how far apart the
   dates and amounts are.
   When a bank row has exactly the same amount on the same day (or a day either side) as your split, it is offered even if the two names have nothing in
   common, and the card says "Same amount and date as this split expense". If
   two bank rows fit equally well, none is offered, and a bank row another
   split already uses is never offered.
4. Select **Match** to link them, or **Keep separate** if they are different
   purchases.
5. Select **Back** when you are done.

![Review matches compares a manual split with an imported bank row](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-matches.webp)

A split can be linked to only one bank row.

## Track a trip in another currency

1. Open **Splits** and select **New** in the cream tile beside the groups.
2. Type the **Group name** at the top of the card (for example "Seoul trip")
   and choose the **Group currency** (for example KRW). The chips under the
   name show what you have chosen.
3. Choose the **Purchase source**: **Cash only** for cash spending, or
   **Bank/card** for card purchases you will later match to your statement.
   Two groups (one of each) keep things simplest.
4. Select **Save group**.
5. Add expenses in the trip currency with **+ Add expense**.

![Create group with a name and a trip currency](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-create-group.webp)

Inside a trip group every amount is shown in that currency (for example
"Serene owes you JP¥26,730" on the demo's Japan trip), and the category donut
counts only that currency.
Different currencies are never added together. A card expense can wait as
"Card statement pending" until the statement row arrives. Outside Splits,
the linked card row counts in dollars, split the same way.

## Settle up a group

1. Open **Splits** in your person view and select the group.
2. Select **Settle group** on the group card.
3. The card at the top reads who paid whom ("Serene paid Ethan") and holds
   the **Amount**, filled in with the balance: type over it to pay a different
   amount. Below it, check **Paid by**, **Received by** and the **Date**, add
   a **Note**, and choose how it was paid under **Paid using**.
   **Currency** and **Evidence** are in the same list.
4. Select **Save settlement**.

![Record settlement for one group](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-settle-group.webp)

The group's current batch closes and moves to **Archived batches**. New
expenses start a fresh batch. Other groups are not touched.

## Simplify settlement across groups

When you have several open groups in the same currency, **Simplify
settlement** works out one amount to pay for all of them.

1. Open **Splits** in your person view and select **Simplify settlement** (at
   the top right of the page on a computer, a row on the group card on a
   phone).
2. The **Simplified settlement** card shows who pays whom and how much. If
   the groups cancel each other out, nothing needs paying. **View included
   activity** marks the expenses it covers.
3. When the bank transfer appears, choose it in **Match a transfer…** and
   select **Match transfer**.
4. Already paid, but the transfer is not in the app yet? Select **Mark paid**.
   It moves to **Settled, awaiting bank match**; open that later and select
   **Match bank transfer**.

![A simplified settlement waiting for its bank transfer](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-simplify.webp)

Undo options: **Undo paid** if you marked it paid too early; **Undo
simplification** to put all its expenses back into the open balance.
Different currencies are always settled separately.

## Undo a settle-up or simplification

Expenses that are part of a settle-up or a simplified settlement are locked:
their amount, currency, shares, payer, date and group cannot change, and they
cannot be deleted. Otherwise the settled amount would silently stop matching.

Select a settled expense and it opens with nothing to change. A line at the
top says when it was settled or simplified and the button under it undoes it:

1. Select **Undo settle-up** (for a settled group) or **Undo simplification**
   (for a simplified settlement). You can also open the batch in **Archived
   batches** and undo it there.
2. The same card turns editable: make your change and select **Save expense**.
3. Settle again for the new balance.

If a change reaches a settled split another way (for example an entry on
Entries), it is refused with the same message and button, and nothing changes.

## Restore a deleted split

1. Open **Splits** and select **History** on the group card.
2. Find the deleted expense or settle-up.
3. Select **Restore**. It comes back with the same shares, group, currency and
   bank link.

![Activity history lists deleted splits with a Restore button](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/splits-history-restore.webp)

While a split is deleted, its entry counts in full for the person who paid.
If the split was already restored in another tab, the history says so and
nothing is restored twice.

## Record Apple Pay purchases automatically

On an iPhone, a Shortcut can save each Apple Pay purchase as an entry and
open it for you to check.

1. Open **Settings → Apple Pay shortcut** on your iPhone.
2. Select **Default account** and put the card you use most first. If the
   shortcut doesn't name an account, entries go to the first account in the
   list. Changes save straight away. **How to set it up** lists the steps
   below.
3. Select **Install Apple Shortcut**. The app copies your private connection
   and opens the Shortcut.
4. Select **Add Shortcut** (or **Replace** if you installed it before).
5. When Apple asks for the connection, paste it. The full address should stay
   visible.
6. In the Shortcuts app, open (or create) the **When I tap Wallet
   transaction** automation and choose **Run Immediately**.
7. Make the automation build a Dictionary with `value`, `merchant` and
   `name` from the transaction.
8. In its final **Run Shortcut** action, choose **Monies Map Apple Pay API**
   and pass it the Dictionary.

![The Apple Pay shortcut section in Settings](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/settings-shortcut.webp)

After a purchase, a notification shows the merchant, amount and account, and
the new entry opens on [Ask](#ask) as a saved card, marked **Apple Pay ·
provisional**. If it landed in
**Other**, Ask suggests a few categories to tap; it also offers to share it
on Splits, and **Open this entry** opens it in Entries to change anything
else.

An entry from Apple Pay, or one you type in on the day, also shows the time
of day it was made (for example "2:32 pm") beside its account, so you can
tell the day's purchases apart and match them to your bank or phone; a bank
file has no time, so those entries show none. To change it, open the entry
and use **Time** under **More details** (**Clear time** removes it).

Paying abroad in another currency (for example with an Amaze card in Bali)
also works: the purchase is saved in the card's currency at the day's
exchange rate, and its note keeps what you paid, such as "Paid IDR 159,000".
Your card's own amount may differ a little. When you import the card's
activity export, each of these purchases is matched to the card's row and
takes its current amount; a later export updates it again if the amount
changed, and the statement sets the final amount. It is never added twice.
If Apple Pay saved a hold's amount (a hotel deposit, or a ride held before
a discount) and a later export no longer lists that hold, the preview asks
which row is the final charge: pick **Same purchase** to move your purchase
onto it, or leave **Not in this file** and it goes back to the amount you
saved.
Before you import, the matched row says what will change, for example
"Updates your estimate for IDR 159,000 from SGD 11.34 to the card's SGD
11.66". After the import, the Done card counts any entries the bank no
longer lists that were removed.

Apple Pay doesn't tell the app which card paid, so a purchase abroad would go
to your first card in **Default account**. On the **Other currencies go to**
row, choose the card such purchases really land on, for example the
Citi card your Amaze card charges. It saves straight away; **Same as other
purchases** turns it off. The category comes from your
category rules (otherwise Other). Each iPhone installs its own copy.

### One phone for each person

Each person has their own phone connection, with its own private key, its own
**Default account** list and its own **Other currencies go to** card. A
purchase from Joyce's phone is saved as Joyce's, on her own cards, and it
shows with her colour in Entries; Tim's phone does the same for Tim. A phone
cannot save a purchase as someone else, even if the shortcut is edited.

To set up a second phone:

1. On that person's phone, open **Settings → Apple Pay shortcut**.
2. On **Who is this phone for?**, choose that person. The list under
   **Default account** starts with their own cards; put the card they use most
   first.
3. Select **Install Apple Shortcut for** that person and follow the steps
   above on their phone.

The first person's phone keeps working as it is: nothing to reinstall. To
turn a phone off (a lost phone, or one that is no longer used), choose its
person, open **More shortcut settings** and select **Stop this phone**. Only
that phone stops; install the shortcut again to turn it back on with a new key.

Keep the private connection secret like a password: do not share screenshots
of it. If it leaks, choose that person, generate a new key under **More
shortcut settings** and reinstall on their phone; the old key stops working at
once and nobody else's phone is affected. If you used an older "Register Apple Pay transaction" Shortcut,
remove it so one purchase cannot create two entries.

### Was this on that card?

When a new entry (from Apple Pay, or one you describe to Ask) is at a shop you
have already paid several times in the last 12 months, and nearly all of those
were on a different card, Ask asks one question, for example "Past Grab entries
were on Citi Rewards. Was this on that card too?" Choose **Yes, Citi Rewards**
to move it, or **No, keep UOB One**. It asks only when at least 3 earlier
entries were at the shop and at least 70% of them were on that one card. Transfers,
settle-ups and purchases in another currency are left out, and it never moves
anything unless you choose Yes. A purchase that a bank statement has already
confirmed, or one on Splits, is not offered.

## Choose your payment ways

When you describe an entry in words and say how you paid, for example
"lunch at nasi lemak bmv 7.20, paynow, mine", the app needs to know which
account that money came from. **Settings → Payment ways** has a block per
person with two rows:

- **PayNow and bank transfers**: the bank account your PayNow, FAST, GIRO,
  NETS and bank transfers come from. It starts on your own first bank
  account in **Default account**. You can pick any bank account,
  including a joint one.
- **Cash**: your cash wallet. To choose one, first add an account of the
  type Cash under **Accounts**.

Each choice saves straight away. Choose **None** if you don't want one.

![Payment ways in Settings, one card per person](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/settings-payment-ways.webp)

The word you typed ("paynow", "cash") is left out of the description. The
person is the one you name ("Serene paid by paynow"), or you when you say
"mine" or name no one. If you name an account ("paynow from POSB joint"),
that account is used. If that person has no cash wallet chosen, the app
asks which account the cash came from instead of putting it on a card. If
no PayNow account is chosen, the entry goes to the first account in
**Default account**, unless that is a credit card; then it asks
too. It never uses someone else's account. Words like "fast food", "badminton nets"
or "cash withdrawal" are read as part of the description.

## Use Money insights

Summary, Month, Entries and Splits each show **Money insights**: one thing
worth knowing about the figures on screen, a calm way to think about it, and
a line that zooms out. In a person view they talk to that person (for example
"Serene owes you JP¥26,730 from the Japan trip."). The household view talks
about shared patterns and never compares the two of you.

A small label says what kind of line it is:

- **To do**: something small that keeps your numbers right or settles
  something. For example: a card statement that differs from what's
  recorded here by $42.80, a bill you planned that isn't matched to its
  payment yet, a month whose plan spends more than the income you expect
  (the savings goal is already part of that spending), money owed on a
  shared cost, or purchases with no category
  yet (they sit under "Other" until you pick one). A card bill is one too:
  from a week before its due date while no payment to the card is recorded
  ("Your OCBC 365 Credit Card bill of $33.15 is due in 3 days"), and for a
  month after it while less than the minimum is paid. A payment from a bank
  account you haven't imported yet only shows once you import it. Your bank
  accounts get a few more:
  - your salary started going into another account, while regular payments
    (like an investment GIRO) still come out of the old one;
  - a regular payment didn't go through and the bank charged for trying;
  - a regular payment coming up within a month won't fit in the account,
    going by its latest statement;
  - money you moved between your own accounts is counted as income;
  - purchases from a trip abroad are still under "Other";
  - on Splits, a payment between the two of you matches a settlement that's
    waiting to be matched.

  These come first, because every other number depends on them. On Month, a month's
  **Something to think about** comes before a card's statement difference,
  which then appears under **Also this month**.
- **Something to think about**: a pattern worth an honest decision, such as
  one large cost that took the month past what you planned. There is never
  more than one at a time.
- **Worth knowing**: something that may well be fine but is easy to miss:
  subscriptions adding up, a category slowly going up, a possible double
  charge, a late charge, interest or annual fee from the last two months
  that the bank hasn't refunded, a card whose latest statement shows a
  penalty interest rate while money is owed on it, or a bank account's own
  charges (a charge for a failed payment, or a monthly fee that has come
  three months in a row).
- **Going well**: money left in the plan, months under plan, savings on
  track.
- **Fun fact**: a light line from your own spending, such as your regular
  spot or the priciest thing on a trip. It never sits next to a
  **Something to think about**.
- **Big picture**: a quieter line that zooms out: how much of your income
  you kept over the year, how many months your bank balances would cover,
  or how much of the month's income your bills and subscriptions take.

When a line needs one of the app's own words, it explains it in the same
sentence, for example matching a bill to its payment ("linking") or paying
back a shared balance ("settling up").

1. Read the bold line and the line under it.
2. If a link sits under them (**Compare with the statement**, **Show these
   purchases**, **Show these entries**, **Show the payment**, **Settle
   group** or **Review matches**), select it to open exactly those records,
   in the month they're in. On Entries the link goes once the list already
   shows just those records.
3. Select **See all insights** for up to three more things worth knowing
   and now and then a short quote.
4. Tap any insight (the bold line, a line under **See all insights**, the
   **Fun fact** or the **Big picture**) and choose **Ask about this** in the
   small menu that opens, to have it explained on [Ask](#ask): the question
   "Explain: …" and a card with what the line is based on (the amounts,
   wallets, months and dates it used), the entries behind it, and the
   line under it. Its buttons open the right place: **Open in Entries**
   for exactly those entries, and, depending on the line, **Compare with
   the statement**, **Open Month**, **Open Splits** or a question Ask
   already answers, such as "How am I doing against the plan?". When
   Claude is set up it may add a sentence or two in its own words, using
   only those figures, marked **Worded by AI**. With money totals hidden
   the lines can't be tapped, and an answer already on Ask hides its
   figures and words.

Money insights can be folded. Under the title, a card shows the
first thing worth knowing (a **To do** comes first): its label, its headline
and the start of the line beneath it, with "+2 more" and an arrow when there
are others. Select the card to open all of it, and select the
title row to fold it again. On a phone it starts folded; on a computer it
starts open. With money totals hidden, the card just says how many insights
there are. Each choice is remembered in this browser, separately for phone and
computer.

Money insights change month by month. Each month they word the same fact a
different way and pick a different Fun fact and quote, so none of
these comes back within a year on the same page. Something that is true
almost every month, like your subscriptions or your five largest payments,
only leads when it is unusual (your subscriptions changed, or a few one-off
buys shaped the month); otherwise it waits under **See all insights**, or
comes up now and then. A month with nothing unusual says so in one calm
line. Coming back to the same
month, they give other lines a turn once one has been shown and start with
what changed ("Down from $42.80 since your last visit.").
When you fix something they say "Sorted" once. Early in the month they look
ahead at bills coming up, mid-month at how spending is pacing, and at the
end they wrap up. If nothing has changed since you last looked, they say so
in one short line. What they have shown you is remembered only in this
browser; nothing about your visits is saved anywhere else.

![All insights expanded](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/summary-insight-expanded.webp)

Money insights use the app's own figures and wording, and never change the
figures. They never
guess what you'll spend or tell you what is safe to spend; money left after
spending still has to cover bills, transfers and savings. The one time they
look ahead is for a regular payment coming up from a bank account: they
compare it with the account's latest statement, say which statement they
used, and only ever speak up when it won't fit. They are only as up to date
as your last import.

2. Fill in **Display name**, **Institution**, **Owner**, **Account type** and

3. Set the **Opening balance**: the balance just before your first imported

4. Select **Create account**.

## Manage categories and category matching

**Categories** holds every category with its icon and colour
([Default categories](#default-categories)). You can also change a category's
icon and colour by selecting its icon on Summary.

**Category matching** rules categorise imported rows before you commit:

1. Open **Settings → Category matching**.
2. Add a rule with the merchant text, the category and a priority.
3. Rules ignore capital letters, spaces and punctuation, and match any part
   of the bank text. Separate words with commas when all must appear: for
   example `paynow-fast, lunch` matches only rows containing both. Very short
   words must appear on their own. Lower priority numbers are checked first.

![Category matching shows overlapping rules to review](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/settings-category-rules.webp)

**Money back lowers spending.** When money comes in under a spending
category, such as a refund or a friend paying you back for dinner, it lowers
that category's spending instead of counting as income. Money in under
Salary, Extra Income, Other - Income, Savings, Investments, Transfer or Other
stays income, so put a gift you received in Extra Income.

When you keep re-categorising similar rows, the app suggests a rule (a
number appears on the Settings tab). For each suggestion choose **Add rule**,
edit it first, or **Ignore**. Overlapping rules are listed first so you can
tidy them. Rules file new imports. When a rule matches past entries that are
still in Other or Other - Income, they are listed under **Past entries your
rules now match**; choose **Move** to file them. Entries already in another
category are never changed.

## Review unresolved transfers

A transfer between your own accounts (for example paying a card from savings)
should appear twice: out of one account and into the other. Until both sides
are paired, balances can look wrong.

1. Open **Settings → Unresolved transfers**.
2. Choose a month; the list is paged, so a backlog can be worked through one
   statement period at a time.
3. Select **Manage transfer** to pair it, or **Open in entries** to fix the
   row.

**Balance trust rules** explains the three habits that keep balances
trustworthy: a correct opening balance, a checkpoint for each statement
month, and paired transfers. It also keeps **Reconciliation exceptions**: a
list of known gaps (a missing bank row, a timing difference) that should not
be forgotten. Adding one does not change any balance.

![Balance trust rules](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/settings-trust.webp)

## Find an entry: filters, search and months

1. Open **Entries** and choose the month with **‹** and **›** at the top
   right (on a phone, use the buttons by the View and scope bar).
2. Type in **Search**: merchant, note, wallet or amount.
3. Narrow with **By wallet**, **By category** and **By type**.
4. Select **Reset filters** to see everything again.

From Summary, the **›** beside a category, then **View entries**, opens

# Troubleshooting

## The app stays on Loading

While a page loads, the app shows a pale outline of it, so nothing jumps
when your figures arrive. When you move to another page, the old one stays
until the new one is ready, with a small "Loading latest data..." note near
the top. If a step takes too long, the app stops waiting and shows which part
failed, with a button to try again.

1. Select the retry button.
2. If it keeps failing, check your internet connection and reload the page.
3. On the private app, you may need to sign in again; reload and follow the
   sign-in page.
4. Still stuck? **Settings → Error diagnostics** keeps the details of failed
   requests for whoever looks after the app.

## "The Month page could not load" (or another page)

The page you opened could not get its data; the message names that page, for
example "The Imports page could not load." Your saved data is not affected.
The page shows this message instead of old figures, so you never mistake last
month's numbers for this month's.

![A page that could not load, with Try loading again](/faq/guide/desktop/thumbs/page-load-error.webp)

Select **Try loading again**. A draft you had open is kept.

## "This page could not refresh. Saved changes are kept."

Your save worked, but the page could not reload the updated totals. Some
figures may be out of date until it refreshes.

- Select **Refresh now** to try again, or **Dismiss** to carry on.
- Moving to another page or month also clears it, because that page loads
  fresh data.

## "This page hit a problem and could not be shown"

Something went wrong while drawing the page. Your saved data is not affected.
Select **Try again**, open another page, or **Reload app**. If it says the
page needs a fresh copy of the app, reload: a new version was published.

## I can't change a split

The split is part of a settle-up or a simplified settlement, so its amount,
shares, payer, date and group are locked. See [Undo a settle-up or
simplification](#undo-a-settle-up-or-simplification). The same applies when
you edit a shared entry on Entries: a change that would move the settled
split is refused, with the same undo button.

## I can't change a statement-confirmed entry

An entry marked **Statement certified** was confirmed by a bank statement, so
its date, description, wallet and amount are locked, and so is whether the
money came in or went out. You can still change the category, note, owner and
splits. The line under the entry card says so in short: "Bank details are
locked to the statement. Category, owner, note and splits stay editable."
Point at (or, with a screen reader, move to) the **Statement certified** chip
for the full rule.

The type can still change within the same direction. Money in can be
**Income** or **Transfer** in, for example a credit from your own account
that was filed as income. Money out can be **Expense** or **Transfer** out.
Changing money in to money out (or the other way) is not possible, because
the statement balance depends on it.

If the bank details really are wrong, the statement import was probably
mapped to the wrong account or needs correcting: roll back the newest
statement for that account and import it again ([Undo an import](#undo-an-import)).
Renaming an account is only right when the account itself was named wrongly.

## An import was rejected or my file is not supported

- **Nothing happens or "Import needs attention"**: check the file is one of
  the supported types ([Which file do I download?](#which-file-do-i-download-from-my-bank))
  and that you chose the right **Default account** first.
- **A row description is blocked as too long or containing page text**: the
  reader merged part of the statement's small print into a row. Nothing was
  saved. Try a clean download of the file.
- **Unknown accounts need mapping before commit**: choose an account for each
  statement account, or **Create account**.
- **Unknown categories**: choose **Categorize as Other**, or map them.
- **Commit failed**: "No partial transactions were kept." Nothing was saved,
  so you can try again. For a very large file, import it in smaller parts.
- **Statement blocked, needs an earlier statement**: import the missing month
  first. Statements build on each other.

## Dates look one day off

The app's calendar is Singapore time. "Today" and the default date on new
entries follow the date in Singapore, wherever you are. Entry, month and
statement dates are plain calendar dates and never shift.

Card entries can have two dates: the day you paid (**Date**) and the day the
bank posted it (**Posted date**). Entries sorts by the day you paid;
statement checks use the posted date. A purchase on the last day of a
statement can therefore land on the next statement.

## A wallet balance looks wrong

1. Check the account's **Opening balance** in **Settings → Accounts**.
2. Check that the latest statement has a checkpoint (Reconcile).
3. Pair **Unresolved transfers**.
4. Look at **Recent imports** for overlapping imports.
5. Use **Compare statement** against the bank PDF.

A balance is opening balance plus income and transfers in, minus spending and
transfers out, so a missing, doubled or misdirected row explains most gaps.

## Are my bank files stored?

No. PDF statements and bank exports are read inside your browser. Only the
rows you commit, the import details and the statement balance are saved.
Refreshing the page or selecting **Start over** clears the draft. An import
saves every row or none of them.

# Glossary

## Words used in the app

- **Actual**: what really happened, taken from your entries.
- **Planned**: what you intended, from the Month plan.
- **Variance**, or the **under plan** and **over plan** amount at the top:
  planned minus actual. Under plan means you spent less than planned.
- **Bill or dated payment**: a fixed commitment (savings, loan, subscription) whose
  actual comes from the entries you link to it.
- **Monthly budget**: a flexible category budget whose actual comes from all
  entries in that category.
- **Entry**: one transaction in your ledger (the list on Entries).
- **Wallet** or **account**: a bank account or card you track.
- **Owner**: the person an entry or account belongs to.
- **Direct**: a person's own entries that are not shared.
- **Shared**: an entry that is on Splits. The person's shared amount is their
  share of it.
- **View**: whose money you see: Household or one person.
- **Scope**: what counts in a person view: Direct, Shared or Direct + Shared.
- **Split**: a shared expense, with each person's exact share.
- **Group**: a set of splits settled together, such as a trip. Each group has
  one currency.
- **Settle-up**: a payment between the two of you that clears a group's
  balance.
- **Batch**: a group's open splits since its last settle-up. Settled batches
  move to Archived batches.
- **Simplification** or **simplified settlement**: one combined amount for
  several open groups in the same currency.
- **Mark paid**: say a settlement was paid before its bank transfer appears.
- **Import**: bringing in a bank file. Each import is kept as a batch you can
  roll back.
- **Statement**: the bank's official monthly PDF.
- **Activity export** or **mid-cycle export**: a file of recent transactions
  you download between statements. Useful, but not final proof.
- **Statement checkpoint**: the bank's closing balance for one account and
  one statement period.
- **Reconcile**: prove your ledger matches the statement's closing balance.
- **Manual provisional**: an entry you typed (or Apple Pay created) that no
  bank file has confirmed yet. One that Apple Pay or Ask saved says so:
  **Apple Pay · provisional** or **Ask · provisional**, with **Added by**
  under **More details**, and a small mark beside its amount in the list.
- **Import provisional**: an entry from an activity export, not yet confirmed
  by a statement.
- **Statement certified**: an entry a bank statement has confirmed. Its bank
  details are locked.
- **Transaction date**: the day the purchase happened (shown as Date).
- **Posted date**: the day the bank recorded it. Statements close by posted
  date.
- **Value date**: the date a bank account movement counts for the balance
  (used by OCBC 360 activity files).
- **Exact, probable and near match**: how sure the app is that an imported
  row is already in your ledger, from certain to worth checking.
- **Already covered**: an imported row the app skipped as a duplicate.
- **Transfer**: money moving between your own accounts. It is neither
  spending nor income.
- **Unresolved transfer**: a transfer whose other side is not paired yet.
- **Reconciliation exception**: a known gap you are tracking until it is
  explained.
- **Money insights**: one thing worth knowing about the figures on each
  screen, with a way to think about it and a long view.

## Default categories

A new household starts with these categories. You can change their icons and
colours, and add your own.

:::categories
